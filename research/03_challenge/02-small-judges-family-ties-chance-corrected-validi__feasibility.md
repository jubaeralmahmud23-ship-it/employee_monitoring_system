## 2. Labels and measurements: are they adequate for the intended claim?

- **RQ1 (chance-corrected validity).** MT-bench gives human votes with ties; LLMBar and JudgeBench give expert/objective binary labels; RewardBench gives constructed labels. Cohen's kappa and exact agreement are computable on all four. Krippendorff's alpha between judges and multiple humans needs multi-rated items, which exist only in MT-bench (count not shown in metadata). The published GPT-4 verdicts (`gpt4_pair`) provide a reference judge without any API call. Pre-registration must fix tie handling, the kappa variant (2- or 3-category), and which human votes count as ground truth.
- **RQ2 (calibration-set curves).** Lee et al.'s method (full text read) needs per-item LLM judgments plus human labels to estimate sensitivity and specificity; the paper's real-data experiment uses GPT-4.1-mini on Chatbot Arena pairs with a 90/10 test/calibration split, and its simulations use n = 1000, m = 200. Calibration subsets of 50/100/200/400 can be drawn from the human-labelled items above by repeated resampling; this is a statistical analysis of existing judgments, not extra compute.
- **RQ3 (same-family effect).** Objective labels (JudgeBench items, gsm8k/MMLU-Pro-style gold answers) let quality be held fixed; family is an observed attribute of the candidate, not randomised, so the estimate is an association adjusted for the measured covariates (quality difference, position, length). The design is sound for that claim; see Section 5.
- **Stated-confidence calibration (ECE)** depends on the judge emitting a usable confidence; with llama.cpp, token log-probabilities of the verdict token are available and give a better-defined confidence than a stated number.
- **Test-retest** needs sampled runs; llama.cpp supports seeded sampling, so this is reproducible.

## 3. Compute and dependency assessment

**Hardware measured today.** 4 cores of an Intel Xeon at 2.10 GHz with avx2, avx512f, avx512_vnni, amx_int8, f16c and fma flags; 15 GB RAM (14 GB free); about 29 GB free disk. gcc 13.3, g++, clang, cmake 3.28 and make are installed, so `llama-cpp-python` 0.3.36 (PyPI, MIT, source build) can be compiled. No numpy, scipy, pandas, torch, transformers, datasets, statsmodels or sklearn are installed; all are on PyPI (statsmodels 0.15.0, krippendorff 0.8.2, datasets 5.0.1, evalci 0.1.0 confirmed). The AVX-512 VNNI and AMX-int8 flags are favourable for llama.cpp integer kernels; this was not benchmarked here because model downloads were out of scope.

**Throughput reference (WebSearch snippets, not measured here).** Qwen2.5-3B Q4_K_M on a 4-core i7-6700 with llama.cpp: prompt processing 35.1 tok/s, generation 12.0 tok/s; Qwen2 3B Q4_K_M on an unspecified 4-thread x86 machine: pp512 67.3 tok/s, tg128 22.7 tok/s. Scaling by parameter count, planning values are: 1.5-2B at Q8 about 60-120 tok/s prefill; 3-4B at Q4 about 35-70 tok/s; 3-4B at Q8 about 25-50 tok/s; generation 10-25 tok/s.

**Per-judgment cost.** A pairwise prompt (system prompt, question, two responses) is roughly 800-2,000 tokens (JudgeBench the longest, LLMBar the shortest); with a verdict-only output format (5-20 tokens) a judgment costs about 10-20 s on a 1.5-2B judge, 18-35 s on a 3-4B Q4 judge and 25-50 s on a 3-4B Q8 judge. Reasoning-on formats add 10-20 s. Prefix (KV) caching of the shared system prompt and the first response for the AB/BA swap reduces but does not remove this. All four cores are used by one llama.cpp process, so judgments do not parallelise further; "wall-clock hours" below equal 4 core-hours each.

**Pilot (recommended before pre-registration).** Two judges (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct Q8_0, Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M), 200 pairs (100 LLMBar, 100 JudgeBench), both orders, verdict-only: 800 judgments at 13 s and 24 s respectively, about 4 h; plus local GGUF conversion and quantisation of two models (about 1 h) and perplexity passes (about 0.5 h). **Estimate: 5-6 wall-clock hours (20-24 core-hours).** The pilot measures the real tokens/s, verifies the chat templates, and sets the subsample sizes.

**Full study as written in the idea.** Stage 1 with 14 judge-quantisation cells, a preregistered subsample (1.5-2B cells on about 2,500 pairs, 3-4B cells on about 1,200 pairs, both orders, plus 200 pairs x 2 orders x 3 sampled runs per cell for test-retest) is about 71,000 judgments, roughly 350 wall-clock hours. Stage 2 generation (5 generators x about 480 prompts) is about 10 h; judging 300 objective prompts x 10 cross-family pairs x 2 orders x 7 judges is 42,000 judgments (about 210 h), which must be cut to about 20,000 (about 100 h) by using fewer prompts or judges. Stage 3 perplexities on the Stage 2 candidates (about 2,400 texts x 7 judges) are about 20-25 h; template and reasoning-on sensitivity on a 300-pair subsample for 7 cells about 60 h. **Estimate for the design as written: 600-750 wall-clock hours (about 25-30 days of continuous background use, 2,400-3,000 core-hours).** A trimmed confirmatory version at the idea's own cap of 20-40k judgments (Stage 1 only, 14 cells, about 1,000 pairs each, no sampled reruns beyond a 100-pair subset) plus a 15,000-judgment Stage 2 is about **250-350 wall-clock hours (10-15 days)**. The idea's text ("several days") underestimates this unless the per-judgment time lands at the fast end of the range; the pilot decides.

**Memory.** The largest file is Qwen3-4B Q8_0 at 4.3 GB plus KV cache; comfortably within 15 GB. **Disk.** The full GGUF set (seven judges x two quantisations) is about 32 GB, above the 29 GB free; models must be downloaded or converted sequentially and deleted after their cells are complete, and local conversion needs the fp16 safetensors (up to 8 GB for Qwen3-4B) temporarily. Both are manageable with a per-model pipeline.

**Mixed-effects logistic regression** (Stage 2) runs in statsmodels (`BinomialBayesMixedGLM`) or, if a frequentist fit is preferred, through a Laplace/PQL approximation; with about 20,000 observations and prompt random intercepts this is minutes on CPU.

**Does the core method need an LLM or embeddings?** Yes: the judges are the object of study. Everything runs with quantised 1-4B open generative models on CPU, which is exactly the regime the paper targets; no embedding model is needed. There is no non-neural variant of the study, but the non-neural baselines (random, always-first, always-longer, lexical overlap) are trivial. Model-call count: about 20,000-40,000 judgments in the trimmed design, up to about 130,000 in the full design, plus about 2,400 generations and about 17,000 perplexity passes.

**Baselines reproducible from public code?** Heuristic baselines: yes, trivially. GPT-4 reference verdicts: shipped in `gpt4_pair`. Published 8B+ kappa values: readable from Norman et al.'s Table 2 (preprint). bayes_evals, the Lee et al. estimator and the Preference-Leakage prompts are on GitHub and must be added to the session; the Lee et al. formulas are printed in their appendix and can be re-implemented if needed.

## 4. Approvals and ethics

No research participants, no new human labelling, no institutional database and no personal data are involved; all human labels come from released benchmarks (cc-by-4.0, MIT, odc-by). No IRB or ethics approval is needed. Model licences: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct carries the Hub tag "other" (its terms were not read here; the others are apache-2.0 or MIT). Gated models (Llama 3.2, Gemma) are excluded, which is a design limitation rather than an approval problem. Releasing judgments, perplexities and prompts is compatible with the benchmark licences provided attribution is kept (ODC-BY, CC-BY).

## 5. What the design can and cannot claim

Can claim: (a) descriptive validity statistics with intervals for named 1-4B judges at two quantisation levels on four released benchmarks, including the exact-match-versus-kappa deflation that Norman et al. report for larger API judges; (b) the calibration-set size needed for the Lee et al. correction to reach a target interval width for these judges; (c) within-item, order-counterbalanced effects of quantisation (same weights, two precisions, same items) which are experimentally manipulated and therefore causal with respect to quantisation; (d) a same-family association in judge verdicts after adjusting for objective quality difference, position and length. 

Cannot claim: that family membership causes the preference (family is observed, not assigned; style, chat template, training-data overlap and length distribution co-vary with it); that perplexity mediates it (Stage 3 is exploratory and mediation from observational covariates is not identified); that MT-bench kappa equals validity against experts (student labellers); anything about open-ended stylistic leakage (objective-label tasks exclude it); or any "first" or "no prior work" statement, since at least one 2026 preprint already scales Qwen3 judges from 1.7B upward (below).

## 6. Blockers and risks (with today's evidence)

1. **Compute time, not capability.** The design as written needs 25-30 days of continuous 4-core use; a pre-registered trimmed design needs 10-15 days. Nothing is infeasible, but the subsample sizes must be fixed from the pilot, and the project timeline must absorb this.
2. **Disk.** 29 GB free versus about 32 GB for the full GGUF set; requires a sequential download/convert/delete pipeline.
3. **Quantisation provenance.** Official repos lack Q4 for Qwen3-1.7B and Q8 for SmolLM2-1.7B; no official Granite GGUF. Either quantise locally with one pinned llama.cpp build (recommended; needs a temporary CPU torch install for `convert_hf_to_gguf.py`) or accept third-party files as a confound.
4. **LLMBar data lives on GitHub.** The HF repo is a loader script that `datasets` 5.0.1 will not run; the princeton-nlp/LLMBar repository must be added to the session, as must sambowyer/bayes_evals, UW-Madison-Lee-Lab/LLM-judge-reporting and David-Li0406/Preference-Leakage.
5. **Overlap to cite and differentiate (new evidence found today).** "When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement: Auditing LLM-as-Judge Reliability" (Yang, Hou and Yang; arXiv 2607.08535, July 2026; the HTML header names a September 2026 conference, so treat as a preprint with a stated venue; abstract and partial HTML read) scales Qwen3 dense judges from 1.7B to 32B on four judgment datasets (names not shown in the HTML received), probes position and verbosity bias and finds "only Qwen3 1.7B to 4B gives a robust adjacent gain". The text received mentions neither quantisation nor chance-corrected metrics, so RQ1's quantisation factor, kappa, calibration curves and the cross-family factorial remain distinct, but the "no record validates sub-4B judges" framing in the idea must be softened to "no record received varies quantisation or reports calibration-set curves for sub-4B judges".
6. **Prior null on self-preference.** "Self-Preference Is Weak or Absent in Verifiable Instruction-Following Revision: A Four-Model Test Under Genuine Authorship" (arXiv 2606.20093, June 2026, abstract) finds no detectable self-preference across four mid-tier families when validity is decided by the IFEval checker (gap -5.1 pp, 95% CI [-12.9, +2.7]); "Self- and Other-Labels Induce Bidirectional Bias in LLM Judges" (arXiv 2608.18091, EMNLP Findings 2026 per the arXiv comment, abstract) finds self-preference disappears under blind evaluation once quality is controlled. Both point toward H4 failing; the idea's framing already treats a null as informative, but the power analysis should be planned for a small effect.
7. **Checks on the idea's own undermining-evidence list.** Norman et al. (full text): 21 judges, all API-served, smallest listed is Qwen 3 8B; the word "quantiz" does not occur; data release is "upon publication", so the 1-4B validity table is not pre-empted. Lee et al. (full text; ICML 2026 per arXiv journal-ref): experiments use GPT-4.1-mini and simulations, no small open judges, so the calibration curves remain a contribution. Thakur et al. (full text; GEM 2025, aclanthology 2025.gem-1.33): includes one sub-4B judge (Gemma 2B) among thirteen, uses percent agreement and Scott's pi on TriviaQA, has an appendix on reference-order sensitivity, and does not cross judge and exam-taker families, so Stage 1 is not a replication and Stage 2 keeps its novelty. Related but different: "A Calibrated Instrument for Measuring How Inference Optimizations Affect Output Quality" (arXiv 2609.18005, abstract) quantises the generator, not the judge, and finds 4-bit indistinguishable from 16-bit at +/-0.3-point resolution, which is a useful prior for H5; BabelJudge (arXiv 2606.22329, abstract) audits a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-4bit judge without human labels; "Judging the Judges: A Systematic Evaluation of Bias Mitigation Strategies" (TMLR 2026 per the arXiv comment, abstract) reports kappa 0.549 for its best API configuration on MT-Bench/LLMBar subsets, an external reference point.
8. **Reference verification** (verify_refs.py, Crossref and arXiv columns): all eight closest studies resolve on arXiv at score 1.0. Crossref matches with score 1.0: Zheng et al. (NeurIPS 2023, 10.52202/075280-2020) and Panickssery et al. (NeurIPS 2024, 10.52202/079017-2197). Crossref matches below 0.85 were checked manually and are wrong (they point to different papers), so "Reliability without Validity" and Tiwari's paper are preprints with no Crossref record; "Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" has no Crossref record found; "Preference Leakage" is ICLR 2026 per its arXiv comment; "Judging the Judges" is GEM 2025 per its arXiv journal-ref. Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex returned 429 throughout.

## 7. Verdict

**feasible-but-slow-on-cpu.** All data are reachable today under permissive licences (one GitHub-hosted set and three GitHub code repositories must be added to the session); all seven ungated judge families fit in memory as GGUF; the toolchain to build llama.cpp exists; no approvals are needed; the statistics are standard. The only real cost is wall-clock time: a 5-6 hour pilot is immediate, a trimmed pre-registered study needs 10-15 days of continuous 4-core compute, and the design as written needs 25-30 days. The claims are supportable as descriptive validity statistics, a within-item quantisation effect, calibration-set curves, and an adjusted same-family association, provided the "no prior work on sub-4B judges" framing is replaced by a differentiation from Yang, Hou and Yang (2026) and from Thakur et al.'s Gemma 2B result, and provided the prior null results on self-preference are cited when H4 is pre-registered.

## 8. Minimum change in resources that would unblock the full design

A single consumer GPU with 8-12 GB VRAM (or a short cloud GPU rental) would bring per-judgment time from 10-50 s to well under 1 s and run the full 130,000-judgment design in one or two days instead of a month; alternatively, a second 4-core CPU box would halve the wall-clock time. About 10 GB more disk would remove the sequential download/delete constraint. Access to the gated Llama 3.2 and Gemma checkpoints (a Hub licence acceptance, not a purchase) would add two model families to the factorial.
