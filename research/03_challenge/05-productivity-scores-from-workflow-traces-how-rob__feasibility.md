## 2. Labels and measurements (adequacy for the intended claim)

- **There is no productivity ground truth in any public log**, and the idea record already says so. Pika et al. had manager expectations and customer feedback for their private log; nothing comparable exists for 4TU logs. The study therefore estimates *reliability across configurations* and *confounding by case mix*, not criterion validity. This is adequate for RQ1–RQ3 as phrased (stability, variance decomposition, audit protocol) and must be stated as the boundary of the claim.
- **Resources are pseudonymous identifiers** (`User_1`, `User_123`; employee numbers in BPI 2015). Whether an identifier is a person, a team account, a shared login or a batch job cannot be verified. BPI 2019 is the exception where the publisher states that batch users are named as such; BPI 2018 describes batch behaviour by human users. The design's heuristics (activity-pattern detection, burstiness, 24h activity, event counts far above the distribution such as `User_1`'s 148,404 of 1.2 M events in BPI 2017) can be *calibrated* on BPI 2019's labelled split and then applied to the other logs, which turns a weakness into a validation step. Sensitivity to inclusion/exclusion of suspected system accounts should be a reported factor.
- **What each measure can mean, given what the logs record.** (a) *Events per active hour* needs start/complete or dense timestamps; BPI 2012 W_ items and BPI 2017 workflow events have lifecycle transitions (per the idea record and the earlier assessment), BPI 2019/2018 are single-timestamp logs where "active hour" depends entirely on the idle-gap threshold — this is the factor the design already varies, and the correct interpretation is "activity density", not labour time. (b) *Completed cases attributed* depends on the attribution rule (first/last/majority touch), which is why the design crosses it; in BPI 2019 a case is a purchase line item touched by several users, so attribution ambiguity is large by construction. (c) *Mean activity processing time* is meaningful only where start and complete events exist for the same activity instance and the same resource; elsewhere it is inter-event time, which conflates waiting and working (Pika et al. note that their workload RBIs "only yield a rough estimation"). (d) *Rework share* and *deadline adherence* require a definition per log (repeated activity within a case; SLA fields exist only in ticketing logs such as BPI 2013/2014 if present). (e) Pika's RBIs are defined per time slot, and the paper itself says "The time series sampling rate is an important parameter that can affect the analysis results" without evaluating it — the window factor in this design operationalises exactly that sentence.
- **Case-mix covariates are available but partial.** BPI 2017 (amount, goal, type), BPI 2019 (item category, subsidiary, vendor, spend class), BPI 2013 (product, impact, team), BPI 2015 (SUMleges, sub-process, municipality). "Difficulty" is never recorded directly; the activity-stratified adjustment (compare a resource only within activity type) is the covariate-free fallback and should be the primary adjustment, with regression on case attributes as the secondary one.
- **Role or group fields** exist in BPI 2013 (`org:group` per the search result; teams per CEUR), Sepsis (group only), possibly BPI 2020 (roles in the description) and can be discovered with `pm4py.org.discover_organizational_roles` elsewhere. The four-fifths-style ratio across groups is therefore computable in at least BPI 2013 and BPI 2015 (municipalities as groups) and, after role discovery, in BPI 2017/2019. It measures disparity between *work groups*, not between protected demographic classes; no demographic attribute exists in any log, and none should be inferred.
- **Statistical adequacy.** With 150 (BPI 2017) to 607 (BPI 2019) human resources and a minimum-event filter, Kendall tau between two configurations has a standard error of roughly 0.05–0.08 for n = 100–150 resources, so the H1 threshold (median tau < 0.7) is testable; quintile-reclassification rates on 100–150 workers have binomial half-widths of about ±8 points, so H2's 25 % threshold is testable but intervals will be wide on Helpdesk-sized logs. Bootstrap over *cases* (the design's choice) is the right resampling unit because resources are the population under study, not a sample. Placebo configurations with shuffled resource labels give the null distribution of tau and are cheap.

## 3. Compute and dependency assessment

**Does the core method need an LLM or embedding model?** No. Every computation is a group-by over an event table (resource x window x case), rank correlations, bootstrap resampling and ANOVA. No neural model, no API, no GPU. `runs_without_llm: true` is correct.

**Does pm4py handle the resource-level computations?** Yes for the Pika indicators (11 RBIs with `t1, t2, r` arguments), role discovery and social networks. It does **not** provide the study-specific layers (attribution rules, idle-gap activity time, case-mix adjustment, rank-stability statistics, reclassification rates, variance decomposition): ~500–1,000 lines of pandas/scipy/statsmodels code. Because pm4py's RBI functions run once per (resource, slot) and would be slow across 400 configurations x hundreds of resources x many slots, the practical route is to use pm4py for XES parsing (`read_xes` returns a DataFrame in 2.7.x) and role discovery, and as the *reference implementation* for unit-testing a vectorised reimplementation of the same RBIs.

**Local environment measured today (scenario B machine):** Python 3.14.4 at `C:\Program Files\Python314` with pandas 3.0.5, numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1, scikit-learn 1.9.0, statsmodels 0.14.6 and pyarrow 25.0.1 installed; pm4py, lxml and polars not installed. A Python 3.12 interpreter also exists at `C:\Users\User\AppData\Local\Programs\Python\Python312`. **Python 3.14 is workable for pm4py:** pm4py 2.7.23.8 lists the 3.14 classifier, ships a pure-Python wheel, its two binary dependencies have Windows cp314 wheels (`lxml-6.1.3-cp314-cp314-win_amd64.whl`, `cvxopt-1.3.3-cp314-cp314-win_amd64.whl`), and its `stable` extra pins pandas 3.0.5, the version already present. A 3.12 venv is not required; it is the fallback if any later dependency lacks a 3.14 wheel. Use a dedicated venv either way.

**Work decomposition and cost model.** The factorial as written is metric (5) x attribution (3) x window (3) x idle-gap (3) x case-mix (3) = 405 cells per log, but attribution only changes the completed-cases metric and idle-gap only the events-per-active-hour metric, so the number of *distinct* score vectors per log is on the order of 60–100. Each score vector is a pandas group-by over the event table plus a window join: 1–3 s per million events on one core. Bootstrap over cases is cheap if per-(case, resource, window) contributions are precomputed once per log and window, so each replicate is a weighted re-aggregation (≈0.2–0.5 s for BPI 2017-sized tables). Pairwise tau over 405 cells is 82,000 pairs of ~150-length vectors: seconds per replicate; restrict bootstrap intervals to the pre-registered primary contrasts (a few dozen pairs) and compute the full matrix once on the point estimates.

**Parsing (the one memory-sensitive step).** pm4py's lxml-based XES import holds the whole document and then builds a DataFrame. Estimates, not measurements: BPI 2017 (29.7 MB gz, 1.2 M events) ≈ 3–8 CPU-minutes, peak 3–6 GB; BPI 2019 (728 MB XES, 1.6 M events, many attributes) ≈ 10–20 min, peak 6–10 GB; BPI 2018 (158 MB gz, 2.5 M events) ≈ 20–40 min, peak 8–14 GB. Convert each log once to Parquet (pyarrow is installed) so that every later run loads in seconds with a fraction of the memory.

**Scenario A (4 cores, 15 GB RAM, ~30 GB disk, no GPU).**
- *Pilot (BPI 2017 only; full 405-cell factorial; 500 case-bootstrap replicates on the primary contrasts; 20 placebo shuffles; automated-account sensitivity):* parse 0.1 h + distinct score vectors ~0.1–0.3 h + bootstrap ~2–4 h + placebo/ANOVA <0.5 h ≈ **3–6 CPU-hours**; with development reruns, about 2–3 working days wall-clock. RAM fits (peak during the one-off parse ≈ 3–6 GB).
- *Full study (BPI 2012, 2013 incidents, 2014 activity log, 2015 x5, 2017, 2017-Offer, 2019, 2020 x5, Helpdesk, 2018 as stress case; Sepsis and RTF only if a usable resource field appears):* roughly 18 logs, dominated by BPI 2019 and BPI 2018 (each ≈ 8–20 CPU-hours with 1,000 replicates) and by the 10 small logs at ≈ 0.5–1 h each: **≈ 50–120 CPU-hours**, i.e. 1–2 weeks wall-clock on 4 cores with reruns. BPI 2018's parse (peak possibly 8–14 GB) is the only step at risk on 15 GB; mitigate by a streaming `lxml.iterparse` to Parquet or by dropping BPI 2018 (it is a stress case, not a primary log). Disk: raw logs ≈ 1.1 GB (BPI 2019 XES alone 0.73 GB; BPI 2015 five x 41 MB; BPI 2018 158 MB gz), Parquet caches ≈ 0.5–1 GB, results < 1 GB: ~3–4 GB total, well within 30 GB.
- Verdict for A: feasible on CPU; "slow" only in the sense of a one-to-two-week serial run, not in the sense of the idea-01 LLM arms.

**Scenario B (i7-14700KF, 20 cores/28 threads, 31.9 GB RAM, RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB, 626 GB free).**
- The GPU is irrelevant to this design (no tensor workloads; cuDF would help only marginally and is Linux-oriented). ## 4. Approvals and ethics

- **Participants:** none. No surveys, interviews or institutional data access. **No IRB/ethics approval is required** for secondary analysis of public, pseudonymised logs; Pika et al. note that their own case-study analysis of an anonymised dataset "was exempt from ethical review" under the Australian national statement, while their survey and human-participant components needed clearance — the present design has only the former kind of component.
- **Terms:** cite each DOI; respect CC BY-NC for BPI 2020 (no commercial reuse of derivatives), CC BY for BPI 2019, CC0 for BPI 2018; read `General_terms_of_use.pdf` (4TU article 12721292, CC0, 191 KB) before redistributing any event-level derivative of the 4TU-terms logs.
- **Re-identification and dignity of pseudonymous workers.** The study produces "worst performer" lists for real pseudonyms of real employees of identifiable organisations (a Dutch bank, Volvo IT Belgium, Rabobank, a Dutch university, five Dutch municipalities whose employee numbers are consistent across logs). Although identifiers are pseudonymous, a 2020 LNCS paper in the citing list ("Quantifying the Re-identification Risk of Event Logs for Process Mining") shows re-identification is a live concern, and Pika et al. warn that "a narrow set of resource behaviors may be used to instigate disciplinary action" and that in some countries employee-union permission is needed for such analyses. Recommended norms: publish distributions, rank-stability statistics and reclassification rates, not named per-pseudonym rankings; never attempt to link pseudonyms across logs or to external data; hash resource identifiers again in released tables; state in the paper that the exercise is a measurement audit, not an evaluation of the individuals.
- **Dual use.** The audit protocol could be read as a recipe for building scores; the mitigations are to frame outputs as robustness tests for deployers, works councils and DPOs (as RQ3 does) and to release the robustness-certificate rules rather than a scoring dashboard.

## 5. What the design can and cannot claim

**Can claim (internally valid):**
- For each public log, the *observed* instability of worker rankings and quintile memberships across a pre-registered space of defensible scoring configurations, with case-bootstrap intervals, and a variance decomposition attributing instability to metric, attribution, window, idle threshold and case-mix choices. This is a descriptive-causal statement about *configurations* (the experimenter varies them; the data are fixed), so it supports "changing choice X reorders Y % of workers in log Z".
- Whether case-mix adjustment systematically reorders workers who handle harder cases, in the logs with case covariates (BPI 2017, 2019, 2013, 2015), and whether disparities between work groups change under adjustment (the four-fifths analogue), in logs with group fields.
- A reusable, tested audit artefact and a calibration of the automated-account detector against BPI 2019's publisher-labelled batch users.
- A conceptual link between measurement reliability and the "rating" mechanism of algorithmic control that does not depend on the direction of the result: convergence (tau > 0.9) is a reassurance finding, divergence is the warning finding; both are informative, as the idea record says.

**Cannot claim (or must be scoped):**
- Criterion validity: nothing shows which configuration, if any, tracks real productivity; the logs have no outcome labels. The claim is about *reliability and confounding*.
- That the pseudonymous resources are individual humans. The study must report results with and without suspected system accounts and acknowledge shared or team logins.
- That workflow-system logs behave like employee-monitoring telemetry. BPI/ITSM logs are the closest public proxy (ticketing systems are themselves a monitoring surface for service desks), but keystroke-, application- and screenshot-based scores are not represented; the paper should say "scores a monitoring vendor would compute *from the same workflow events*", which is a real product category (process-mining dashboards), not a claim about all monitoring software.
- Generalisation beyond the logs analysed. With 15–18 logs from 8–10 organisations the paper can describe heterogeneity and report whether the direction replicates, not estimate a population parameter; cross-log statements should be framed as a multi-case replication.
- Anything about protected-class discrimination. The "four-fifths" analogue compares work groups or roles, not demographic groups, and should be named accordingly to avoid implying an EEOC-style finding.
- The timing-based metrics in single-timestamp logs (BPI 2019, 2018, 2020) measure event density, not labour time; the paper must label them so.

## 6. Blockers

1. **Resource-field presence is confirmed only for some logs.** Publisher text confirms resources for BPI 2019 (with batch/human split), BPI 2015 and BPI 2018 (users with batch behaviour), the idea record's own inspection confirms BPI 2017, and the CEUR summary plus a search-result summary confirm teams/`org:group` for BPI 2013. BPI 2012, 2014, 2020, Helpdesk and RTF are "not shown" and must be checked by opening the files (minutes of work once downloaded). Sepsis has groups only and randomised absolute timestamps; it should be dropped from the ranking analysis. Not a hard blocker: a design-time check.
2. **The 4TU v2 API was down (502) all day** and the CFF endpoint intermittent; downloads should be scheduled with retries, and the terms-of-use PDF has not been read (needed only before redistributing derivatives).
3. **Pika et al.'s ProM plug-in is not retrievable** (401 at yawlfoundation.org), so the baseline cannot be reproduced from the original code. pm4py's documented re-implementation of 11 RBIs, each labelled with Pika's numbering, is a faithful and citable substitute; the DEA productivity module is not in pm4py and would need a small reimplementation if wanted (scipy `linprog` suffices).
4. **Prior-art check is incomplete.** No title among the 64 OpenAlex works citing Pika 2017 is a rank-stability or sensitivity study (closest by title: "Mining context-aware resource profiles in the presence of multitasking" (AI in Medicine 2022), "Unveiling Use Cases for Human Resource Mining" (BISE 2024), "Detecting Weasels at Work" (LNBIP 2023), OrdinoR (DSS 2022)); Pika et al. themselves do not test sensitivity to time-slot size or indicator choice, and Pohl et al. is a conceptual taxonomy. But arXiv (429), Semantic Scholar (429 on previous days) and DBLP (bot wall) could not be queried today, so a targeted ICPM/BPM search remains open, as the idea record already requires.
5. **Ethical framing of per-pseudonym rankings** (Section 4) needs a written data-handling rule before any table leaves the machine; not a resource blocker.
6. Minor: pm4py not installed; BPI 2018 parse memory under scenario A; BPI 2019 is a 728 MB uncompressed XES download.

None is a hard blocker; items 1, 3 and 5 are design decisions, 2 and 6 are setup steps, 4 is the standing literature check.

## 7. Verdict

**feasible-now** (under scenario B; **feasible-but-slow-on-cpu** is too strong a caveat even for scenario A, where the full study is ≈ 50–120 CPU-hours, one to two weeks serial).

Reasoning: all candidate logs exist, are reachable today through at least one 4TU endpoint, are licensed for academic reuse (4TU terms, CC BY, CC BY-NC, CC0) and need no approval; at least five logs (BPI 2017, 2019, 2015, 2013, 2018) have publisher- or record-confirmed resource information, and BPI 2019 uniquely supplies a labelled human/batch split that validates the design's automated-account heuristic. The method is pure tabular computation: no LLM, no embeddings, no GPU, no participants. pm4py provides the Pika RBIs, role discovery and social networks; the study-specific layers are modest pandas/statsmodels code. The pilot on BPI 2017 is a 3–6 CPU-hour job (20–40 minutes wall-clock on the 20-core machine), and the full multi-log factorial is 4–10 hours wall-clock on scenario B or 1–2 weeks on scenario A. The intended claim (reliability and confounding of log-derived rankings, framed for oversight of worker-rating systems) is supportable as scoped in Section 5; criterion validity and generalisation to monitoring telemetry are explicitly not claimable and the idea record already concedes both. The main risk is scientific rather than logistical: the reviewer reaction "metrics are unstable, obviously", which the pre-registered thresholds, variance decomposition, placebo nulls and the audit artefact are designed to answer, and the still-open prior-art search in ICPM/BPM venues.

## 8. Minimum change in resources that would unblock the full design

No new resource is needed: the author's local machine (scenario B) already runs the full factorial across all candidate logs in a working day of wall-clock time, and even the earlier 4-core baseline (scenario A) completes it in one to two weeks if BPI 2018 is parsed by streaming or dropped. The only acquisitions that would *strengthen* rather than unblock the study are (a) one log with an outcome or quality field per case (customer feedback, SLA breach, audit result) to add a criterion-validity arm — the ticketing logs (BPI 2013/2014, Helpdesk) should be inspected for such fields first, and a data-sharing agreement with a service-desk operator would be the external route; and (b) access to Pika et al.'s ProM plug-in archive (currently 401) or the authors' cooperation, to reproduce the DEA productivity baseline exactly rather than from the paper's definitions.
