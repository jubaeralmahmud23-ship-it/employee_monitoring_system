# Novelty challenge: "Productivity Scores from Workflow Traces: How Robust Are Log-Derived Worker Performance Rankings to Defensible Measurement Choices?"

Reviewer stance: adversarial (task is to refute novelty). Date of search: 2026-10-05. Idea source: `research/02_candidate_ideas_merged.json`, `ideas[4]` (lens: business-IS, raw idea 4, no merge).

Idea in one sentence: on public 4TU event logs (BPI Challenge 2017 first, then 2012/2019/2020, Helpdesk, Sepsis), compute per-worker (org:resource) performance scores under a pre-registered full-factorial space of defensible scoring configurations (metric x attribution rule x time window x idle-gap threshold x case-mix adjustment), and report how far worker rankings and bottom/top-quintile memberships move across configurations (Kendall tau / Spearman rho with bootstrap CIs, quintile reclassification rates, variance decomposition over configuration factors, four-fifths-style ratios across roles), packaged as an open pm4py "monitoring-metric robustness audit" protocol positioned inside the algorithmic-management and EU AI Act oversight debate (H1 median tau < 0.7; H2 case-mix adjustment flips bottom-quintile membership for > 25% of workers; H3 shorter windows are monotonically less stable).

## 3. Verdict: partially-covered

Reasoning (why not "likely novel", and why not "largely covered"):

1. Inside process mining the specific study appears not to exist, within the search limits. Pika et al. (TMIS 2017) is the natural home for it: they define the indicator space, parameterise the time slot, compare resources with DEA and discuss limitations, yet the full text contains no sensitivity, robustness, stability, reliability or ranking analysis (Section 1 grep). Martin & Beerepoot (BISE) and Kelemen (2022) survey the HR-mining use cases without raising measurement validity. The fairness-aware line (Qafari & van der Aalst 2019; Pohl et al. 2023; the 2026 triage paper) works on classifier outputs or patient-side outcomes, not on the definition of worker scores. Milani & Maggi (2018) note that choosing a performance-analysis approach is hard but do not quantify what the choice does to results. The dblp and Semantic Scholar channels were unavailable, so ICPM/BPM workshop papers without arXiv versions could have been missed, but the Springer-domain and arXiv-domain searches returned nothing closer.

2. Outside process mining the construct, the statistics and the policy framing are all established, under other names, in at least four literatures the idea does not cite. (a) Provider profiling: Adams et al. (NEJM 2010) computed the reliability of individual physicians' scores from administrative transaction records and the share misclassified in tiered systems (22% in two tiers), with RAND sensitivity analyses over methodological choices; this is the idea's quintile-reclassification analysis with a 15-year head start. (b) Teacher value-added: Papay (AERJ 2011) reports rank correlations of 0.15-0.58 across outcome measures and attributes instability to timing and measurement error; Sloat et al. (2018) report inter-model concordance across six specifications. (c) League tables and composite indicators: Goldstein & Spiegelhalter (1996) give rank intervals and case-mix adjustment; Saisana, Saltelli & Tarantola (2005) run variance-based sensitivity analysis of rankings over construction choices, which is RQ2's variance decomposition. (d) Meta-science: Steegen et al. (2016) and Silberzahn et al. (2018) define the multiverse / many-analysts logic, and Mazei & Hüffmeier (2026) are already importing it into I-O psychology. A reviewer from IS, OR or management science will read the idea as "provider-profiling reliability plus composite-indicator sensitivity analysis, ported to event logs". The idea's related-work section must say so.

3. The baseline the idea calls a "dashboard" is partly a straw man. Vendor documentation (ActivTrak, Hubstaff) shows idle thresholds and activity caps as explicit configurable parameters, so vendors already know the scores depend on them; the claim that "none audits what such scores actually measure" is supported for the academic records seen, but the artefact must be compared with what these products expose, and the policy primer (PMC10026198) already argues vendors hide the score logic.

4. Contradicting or complicating motivation to address. (a) Reliability rises with the number of observations (Adams et al.); on BPI Challenge 2017 (1.2 M events over 150 resources) high-volume workers may rank stably under every configuration and instability may be confined to low-volume or part-time resources, in which case H1 (median tau < 0.7) could fail and the headline becomes a reassurance result, as the idea itself concedes. (b) Gu & Koenker (Econometrica 2023) show that selecting top/bottom groups from noisy scores of heterogeneous precision is a compound decision problem with optimal empirical-Bayes rules; naive quintiles are a known-bad baseline, so demonstrating that they are unstable is less informative than comparing them with precision-aware selection. (c) Nakatumba & van der Aalst (2010) show processing speed depends on workload, a confounder absent from the configuration space; case-mix adjustment alone will not remove it. (d) Qafari & van der Aalst already motivate fairness-aware PM with "blaming overloaded employees for delays", so the "overloaded worker misread as slow worker" story is not new to the community.

What was not found after 61 queries, and therefore remains the idea's claimable contribution: no study that (a) treats the scoring configuration of log-derived worker performance measures as a crossed factorial design on public event logs, (b) reports rank-stability, quintile-reclassification and variance-decomposition statistics for it, (c) releases a pm4py-based audit protocol, or (d) links the result to deployer oversight duties under the EU AI Act. These are an application-level transfer and an engineering artefact, not a new construct; "partially-covered" is the appropriate verdict.

Recommendations if the idea proceeds: reframe the contribution as "profiling-reliability and multiverse analysis for event-log worker scores", citing Adams et al., Papay, Goldstein & Spiegelhalter, Saisana et al., Steegen et al. and Silberzahn et al. as antecedents; add per-worker reliability (e.g., split-half or signal-to-noise) and rank intervals so that instability can be separated from small-sample noise; add workload (Nakatumba) as a configuration or covariate; include Gu-Koenker style empirical-Bayes selection as a comparator to raw quintiles; pre-register the exclusion threshold for low-volume resources because it will drive the headline tau; and read the full texts of Adams et al. (NEJM) and Saisana et al. before committing, since this review saw them as snippets and a Crossref abstract respectively.
