# Novelty challenge: idea 02

**Idea (index 1 of `02_candidate_ideas_merged.json`):** Small Judges, Family Ties: Chance-Corrected Validity, Calibration-Set Requirements, Quantisation Cost and Same-Family Preference Leakage for CPU-Runnable 1-4B LLM Judges

**Reviewer stance:** adversarial; the goal was to find work that already does what RQ1 (chance-corrected validity table for quantised 1-4B open judges with AB/BA swaps and test-retest), RQ2 (calibration-set size curves with the Lee et al. correction) and RQ3 (fully crossed generator-family x judge-family design with quality held fixed, perplexity as mechanism) propose.

**Date of search:** 2026-10-03. **Verdict:** partially-covered (see section 4; RQ3 and the "validity table" half of RQ1 are substantially pre-empted, the quantisation factor and the small-judge calibration curves are not).

## 3. What the search did not find

- No paper that varies **4-bit vs 8-bit quantisation of the judge itself** as an experimental factor and reports chance-corrected agreement with human labels. The closest are Awuni et al. (one Q4_K_M stack plus a float16 replication, 7-9B judges) and Wang & Lee (4-bit fine-tuned 1.7B evaluator).
- No paper reporting **calibration-set size curves (50-400 human labels)** for the Lee et al. correction applied to small open judges; Lee et al. use 90/10 splits with API judges, evalstats and Chen et al. treat the estimators generically.
- No **fully crossed family design with judges below 7B**; Awuni et al. stop at 7-9B, Goel et al. at 7-9B, Chen et al. and Pombal et al. at frontier/large models.
- No IS-venue paper (AIS eLibrary query) validating small local judges.

## 4. Verdict: partially-covered

Reasoning:

1. **RQ3 is substantially pre-empted.** Awuni et al. (2609.17857, September 2026) run a fully crossed generator-family x judge-family pairwise design with off-the-shelf, GGUF-Q4 quantised open judges on llama.cpp, no API, a corrected estimator that holds candidate family fixed, Bradley-Terry and human-consensus quality controls, AB/BA swaps, a judge-size contrast, and judge-side log-likelihood as the familiarity channel (61% attenuation). That is the idea's Stage 2 and Stage 3 design with 7-9B instead of 1-4B judges and open-ended instead of objectively labelled prompts. The "quality held fixed by objective labels" move is separately published by Chen et al. (2504.03846, self level, 7 families) and Pombal et al. (2604.06996, family level, verifiable rubrics), and the evaluator-quality null is formalised by Roytburg et al. (ICML 2026). A 1-4B replication is an increment, and the idea's own risk note (small judges near chance makes the family test underpowered) weakens that increment further.
2. **The RQ1 "validation table for 1-4B judges" is partly done.** SLMJury (2606.07810) benchmarks 16 judges from 0.6B to 14B, including Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B and Qwen3-1.7B/4B, with Cohen's kappa among its metrics; Yang et al. (2607.08535) give Cohen's kappa, position-flip and verbosity bias for Qwen3-1.7B and 4B on LLMBar and three other sets; Jayarao et al. (2509.13332) cover Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B/4B on RewardBench with positional-bias conditions; Thakur et al. include Gemma 2B with Scott's pi. None of them uses the Norman et al. protocol in full (human MT-Bench votes, Krippendorff alpha, 3-5 repeated runs in both orders), and none varies quantisation, so the table is incomplete rather than absent. SLMJury's headline ("reliable automated evaluation does not require large proprietary models"; Qwen3-4B at 87.8% on objectively labelled items) also cuts against the idea's H3 expectation that sub-4B judges stay near chance on objective pairs, at least for reference-based verification.
3. **What survives.** (a) The quantisation factor (4-bit vs 8-bit) applied to judges, with chance-corrected validity and swap consistency, is not in any record received. (b) Calibration-set size curves for small judges under the Lee et al. correction (and the comparison with evalstats/PPI estimators) are not in any record received. (c) A 1-4B crossed-family test on objectively labelled items, with the Awuni et al. estimator and Roytburg et al. null as baselines, remains open but is now a replication-and-extension of a September 2026 preprint rather than a first test.
4. Why not "largely-covered": no single paper or tool delivers the bundle (quantisation x size x chance-corrected validity x calibration curves x family crossing for 1-4B judges), and the quantisation and calibration-curve components have no direct precedent. Why not "likely-novel": two of the five stated contributions (the 1-4B validity table and the family-effect factorial) have close precedents published between April and September 2026 that the idea file does not cite.

Recommended repositioning if the idea proceeds: drop "absent from the records received" for the validity table; cite and build on SLMJury, Yang et al. and Awuni et al.; make the quantisation factor and the calibration curves the headline; use the Awuni et al. column-wise estimator and the Roytburg et al. evaluator-quality null in Stage 2; pre-register that Stage 2 is a size-extension of Awuni et al. to 1-4B judges on objectively labelled items.
