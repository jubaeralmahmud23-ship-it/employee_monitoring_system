# Novelty challenge: idea 03

**Idea (index 2 of `02_candidate_ideas_merged.json`):** Gaming the AI Rater: Self-Presentation Cues and Embedded Instructions in LLM-Based Evaluation of Employee Work Products

**Reviewer stance:** adversarial; the goal was to find work that already does what RQ1 (effect of low-effort, worker-controllable cues such as assertive self-assessment, seniority claims, prior-rating anchors, "note to reviewer" instructions and politeness signals on ratings from small local judges, relative to quality sensitivity), RQ2 (rank reversals of gamed low-quality over un-gamed high-quality items) and RQ3 (cheap mitigations for 1-4B judges: metadata stripping, rubric anchoring, reference-guided grading, pairwise with order swap) propose, or that already frames the rated party as a worker under algorithmic control.

**Date of search:** 2026-10-03. **Verdict:** partially-covered (see section 3). The cue-level empirical core of RQ1 and hypotheses H1-H2 are substantially pre-empted by Hwang et al. (2508.07805, persuasion cues, judges down to Qwen2.5 1B and LLaMA 3.2 1B), Kapetanovic et al. (2608.25869, prior-score anchors, CIKM 2026), Maloyan and Namiot (2504.18333, content-author instruction injection on Gemma-3-4B and Llama-3.2-3B, with a recommendation to prefer comparative over absolute scoring) and Mayilvaghanan et al. (2602.14970, "Trainee" vs "Senior Advisor", prior QA scores and politeness cues in an LLM contact-centre QA system). What is not found: a single design that crosses cue type with verified quality and reports the cue effect as a ratio to quality sensitivity, rank-reversal rates against ground truth, and the quality-sensitivity cost of mitigations on CPU-runnable 1-4B judges.

## 3. Verdict and reasoning

**Verdict: partially-covered.** No single work runs the idea's full design, but its empirical core is already distributed over several 2025-2026 papers:

1. **RQ1 cue effects on small judges.** Hwang et al. (2508.07805) embed authority, flattery, consistency and other one-sentence appeals in wrong answers and grade them with Qwen2.5-Instruct from 1B upward (plus LLaMA 3.2 1B per the search snippet), in pointwise and pairwise formats, with a counter-prompting mitigation, and find up to 8% inflation that model size does not remove. Maloyan and Namiot (2504.18333) test embedded direct instructions ("output score 9") on Gemma-3-4B and Llama-3.2-3B as content-author attacks and find small models more vulnerable. Kapetanovic et al. (2608.25869, CIKM 2026) show prior-score anchors move 7 of 8 judges including Llama-3.2-3B and that CoT and warnings do not help. Zhao, Esmaeili and Fard (2604.16790) probe authority/provenance and self-enhancement cues on Qwen3-4B and Qwen2.5-Coder-3B for code with gold labels. Together these cover five of the idea's six cue types on 1-4B judges, leaving mainly the exact "note to reviewer" phrasing and the politeness/effort signal in a work-product setting.
2. **Workforce framing.** Mayilvaghanan et al. (2602.14970) already audit an LLM contact-centre QA system with "Trainee" vs "Senior Advisor", prior QA scores, coaching-note priming and politeness dimensions across 18 LLMs, and Almog, Lippman and Martin (2603.02076) run a worker experiment on behaviour under AI evaluation. The "rated worker under algorithmic control" framing is therefore not unoccupied, although neither paper treats the cue as a strategic act of the rated worker or ties it to Kellogg et al.'s rating mechanism.
3. **H2 (absolute vs comparative) and mitigations.** Maloyan and Namiot recommend comparative over absolute scoring from their defense experiments; RobustJudge (2506.09443) compares 8 defenses under pointwise and pairwise protocols; Zhou (2607.05904) shows commit-first (reference-guided) grading collapses false positives; Soumik (TMLR 2026) compares nine debiasing strategies. The mitigation menu is known; what is missing is its application to 1-4B quantised judges with the cost in quality sensitivity reported.
4. **Metric novelty is partial.** Sahoo et al. (2512.10449) already weight score inflation by decision-shift severity relative to ground truth (WAVS); rank-reversal-style reporting exists for position bias (2305.17926). The explicit gaming-to-signal ratio and cue-driven rank-reversal rate on verified-quality work products were not found.
5. **The structural analogue is an established literature.** In education, the evaluated party (the student) inserting coercive or persuasive text into the graded artefact is already studied with rubric-based graders, human-graded references, score-inflation metrics, panel-disagreement signals and sanitisation filters (2606.03090; EvalHack; SteadyEval; "When AI Is Fooled"; 2512.10415 and its successor). The worker-and-employer version differs in stakes and theory, not in mechanism.
6. **Contradicting evidence the idea must address.** Thornton (2602.16741) finds authority-spoofing comments do not degrade LLM vulnerability detection and that stripping comments hurts weaker models; Deng et al. (2601.08843) find rubric-conditioned Qwen2.5-72B resilient to prompt injection; the Wharton GAIL report (snippet) finds negligible injection effects on most frontier graders; Hwang et al. find no protective size effect, which weakens the "report a model-size trend" argument, while Maloyan and Namiot and the GAIL snippet point the other way.

What would remain novel if the idea is repositioned: (a) a single pre-registered factorial that crosses human-writable cue types with verified quality (executed tests for mutated code, expert-vs-degraded support answers) on CPU-runnable 1-4B GGUF judges; (b) the gaming-to-signal ratio and rank-reversal rate as the headline measures; (c) the quality-sensitivity cost of metadata stripping, rubric anchoring, reference-guided and swapped-pairwise scoring for small judges; (d) the extension of measurement-gaming and algorithmic-control theory to text-level gaming of LLM raters, for an IS audience. The idea should cite 2508.07805, 2608.25869, 2504.18333, 2602.14970, 2604.16790, 2506.09443 (IEEE TIFS 2026), 2606.03090, EvalHack and SteadyEval as direct precursors and drop any framing that implies submitter-side cues are untested.
