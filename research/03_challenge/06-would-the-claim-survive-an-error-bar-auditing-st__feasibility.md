# Feasibility assessment — idea 06

**Idea title:** Would the Claim Survive an Error Bar? Auditing Statistical and Judge-Validation Practice in Published LLM Evaluations in IS and Industry-Track Venues

**Assessed:** 2026-10-05, on the author's local Windows 11 machine. Source record: `research/02_candidate_ideas_merged.json`, `ideas[5]` (lens: governance-evaluation validity; `runs_without_llm: true`).

**Method of this assessment:** 6 WebSearch queries (1 restricted to aisel.aisnet.org; the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted after the sixth, so the remaining checks are API- and curl-based); a Crossref REST sweep of every 2023–2026 journal article in 11 IS journals by ISSN (3,583 records; term matching done locally on title + Crossref abstract, with HTML tags stripped for the Wiley titles); a polite crawl (0.8 s delay, browser User-Agent) of the 109 ICIS 2024/2025 and ECIS 2024/2025 track listing pages on the AIS eLibrary (1,557 paper titles); ACL Anthology volume pages and `.bib` volume files for seven industry-track volumes; arXiv export API by id for eight method/comparator papers; `gh api` for `sambowyer/bayes_evals` and `Shreyaskc/evalci`; PyPI JSON for `evalci`; and the permitted access tests (two requests to link.springer.com, two to aisel.aisnet.org PDFs, one HEAD to an ACL PDF). WebFetch of the AIS eLibrary search page returned 403; curl with a browser UA got the empty search form (results need a JavaScript session), so eLibrary search counts come from the track-page crawl instead. Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex were not used (429 on the day's probes). No paper corpus was downloaded; one Springer PDF (1.65 MB) and one ACL HEAD were fetched to test access depth; no package was installed.

## 2. Labels and measurements (adequacy for the intended claim)

- **No external labels are needed.** Every variable is a coded property of a published paper. The codebook in the idea record (n items; runs/seeds; decoding stated; model name with version or access date; prompts released; SE/CI/significance test; LLM judge used; human validation of judge; agreement metric and chance correction; contamination discussed; code/data availability) is a set of binary or short-categorical items with precedent in the "Awes, Laws, and Flaws" criteria and the 2605.21404 scoring schema; the two should be read before preregistration to align item definitions so that the IS/industry numbers are comparable to theirs.
- **Headline-claim extractability.** The rule "first quantitative comparison in the abstract, else first in the results" is operational. The AIS eLibrary abstracts seen already contain such sentences (ICIS 2025 fact-checking paper: "top-tier LLMs (e.g., GPT-4.5) already beat legacy fact-checking systems"), and ACL industry abstracts routinely state point differences (the ACL 2026 industry volume page shows e.g. "83.9% Task Success Rate (TSR), outperforming its predecessor by 4 percentage points"). The risk is not extractability but **ambiguity in what counts as one claim** (a difference in accuracy vs. a difference in F1 vs. a relative improvement). Fix in advance: code the metric, the two compared quantities, n, and whether n refers to items, cases or users.
- **Share of papers reporting n.** Not measurable from here without reading full texts; in the *CL strata n (test-set size) is customary, in IS it is less reliably stated. The excluded share is itself an RQ1 outcome, as the idea record says. Expect RQ2 to be computable for perhaps 60–80 % of industry-track papers and a smaller, unknown share of IS papers; plan the RQ2 n accordingly (below).
- **Uncertainty models for RQ2 need an extra assumption the papers will not supply.** (a) The naive binomial SE of a difference of two accuracies on the same n items, computed as if unpaired, is an *upper bound* on the paired SE; the true paired SE depends on the item-level discordance rate, which papers do not report. (b) Miller-style clustered SEs need an ICC; the idea's sensitivity range {0, 0.1, 0.3} is the right response, but cluster structure (questions grouped by document, user, task) also has to be coded from the paper and is often absent. (c) The Beta-Binomial comparison in `bayes_evals` can be run in independent or paired mode; without item-level data only the independent mode is honest. Consequently **survival should be reported as an interval (paired lower bound … unpaired upper bound), not a point rate**, and H3 ("fewer than 50 % exceed the Bayesian 95 % interval") should name which mode it uses.
- **Repeated runs and decoding** are binary codes that are easy to apply; "model identifier with version or date" needs a short rule for API model names (e.g. `gpt-4` alone = no version; `gpt-4-0613` or an access date = version).
- **Judge validation items.** "Human validation of the judge" and "chance-corrected agreement statistic" are clear; "raw agreement only" vs "kappa/alpha reported" is exactly the distinction Norman et al. formalise. Expect low base rates, so H2 (<25 % report chance-corrected agreement) is testable with modest n: with 60 judge-using papers and a true rate of 15 %, the Wilson 95 % CI is roughly 8–26 %, so H2 is decidable only if the true rate is well below 25 %.
- **Two coders.** Kappa requires a second coder on at least a 20–30 % sample. The author has no collaborator in the session record; a second coder must be recruited (a fellow student or colleague). Without one, the study can still be done with a single coder plus a documented intra-coder re-test after an interval, but reviewers at BISE/ISJ/JAIS will expect inter-coder reliability.
- **Precision of the venue comparison (RQ3/H1).** With ~40 IS papers vs ~150 industry papers and true rates of, say, 20 % vs 40 % reporting any uncertainty, the difference (20 points) has a 95 % CI of roughly ±16 points — detectable but imprecise; a 10-point difference would not be. H1 should be stated as directional with an interval, as the idea record already concedes.

## 3. Compute and labour assessment

**Compute (both scenarios):** negligible. The analysis is Wilson intervals, closed-form binomial and clustered SEs, Beta posterior sampling (a few thousand draws per claim), a Monte Carlo over ICC values and logistic regressions on a few hundred rows. In Python 3.14 standard library (`statistics`, `random.betavariate`, `math`) the whole re-analysis runs in seconds; numpy/scipy (not installed) would only add convenience. Scenario A (4 CPU cores, 15 GB) and scenario B (i7-14700KF, 31.9 GB, RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB) are equivalent for the design as written; the GPU is irrelevant. Disk: a 300-paper PDF corpus is ~0.5–1.5 GB (ACL PDFs are ~0.4 MB, Springer ~1.6 MB each as observed).

**Labour (the binding resource).** Estimates per paper are for a trained coder with the codebook fixed:

| Task | Per unit | 150-paper design (≈40 IS Springer + 110 ACL industry) | 300-paper design (adds ICIS/ECIS and a larger ACL sample) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Codebook development, pilot on 10 papers, calibration meeting | — | 15–20 h | 15–20 h |
| Title/abstract screening (83 Springer hits + 171 AIS hits + ACL sample) | 2–3 min | 8–12 h (Springer + ACL sample) | 15–20 h (all AIS hits too) |
| Full coding, primary coder (14 items + headline claim with n and accuracies) | 30–45 min | 75–115 h | 150–225 h |
| Second coder on 25 % sample | 30–45 min | 20–30 h | 40–55 h |
| Adjudication and kappa | — | 8–12 h | 15–20 h |
| Re-analysis tool (closed-form + Beta-Binomial + ICC sweep), tests, release | — | 20–30 h | 20–30 h |

| **Total** | | **≈ 150–220 person-hours (6–9 weeks part-time for one person plus 20–30 h from a second coder)** | **≈ 270–390 person-hours (3–4 months part-time)** |

LLM-assisted PDF extraction (scenario B only; see section 4) could cut primary coding time by perhaps a third but adds a validation study of its own (≈15–25 h) and a methodological liability.

**Does the core method need an LLM or embedding model?** No. Every step is human coding plus closed-form statistics. Two places where an LLM would be tempting: (i) screening the 1,557 ICIS/ECIS titles and the 650+ ACL industry papers for the inclusion criterion, and (ii) extracting n, accuracies and the headline sentence from PDFs. Under scenario A there is no local model capable of (ii) at useful speed and no API key, so the question is moot. Under scenario B the RTX 5070 Ti can run a 7–14 B open model locally without any API, so (i) and (ii) become possible. They should nevertheless be **excluded from the confirmatory pipeline or validated against human coding on a ≥50-paper sample with kappa reported**, for two reasons: an audit of how carefully the field validates LLM judges cannot itself rely on an unvalidated LLM judge without inviting the obvious circularity objection; and the items most at risk of extraction error (n, whether a kappa is reported, whether runs were repeated) are exactly the quantities the headline results depend on. Using a local model for *screening only*, with every included paper then human-coded and a random sample of excluded ones checked, is defensible and should be reported as such.

## 4. Approvals and ethics

- **No participants, no personal data, no institutional data.** The units are published papers; the coded variables are properties of the text. No IRB/ethics approval is required. The second coder is a collaborator, not a participant.
- **Naming of papers.** The audit will necessarily identify which papers lack error bars or judge validation. Precedent (the ICSE 2026 reproduction study names the 18 studies it attempted to reproduce; "Awes, Laws, and Flaws" and Bean et al. report aggregates with a released corpus list) supports releasing the *list of included papers* and *aggregate* rates openly, while paper-level codes are released in a form keyed to the paper DOI only if the authors are comfortable being auditable — the idea record's plan to release paper-level codes "only in anonymized form" is a conservative middle position; releasing DOI-keyed codes is more reproducible and is what meta-research venues increasingly expect. Decide and preregister. Do not characterise individual papers' claims as "false"; the finding is that a claim is *not distinguishable from noise under the paper's own n*, which is a statement about reported evidence, not about truth.
- **Licence compliance:** Springer CC BY and ACL CC BY texts may be quoted; AIS eLibrary content read under a personal subscription may be coded but not redistributed.
- **Preregistration** (OSF or similar) is cheap and strengthens the confirmatory hypotheses H1–H3; it also fixes the headline-claim rule before any paper is read.

## 5. What the design can and cannot claim

**Can claim:**
- Prevalence, with Wilson 95 % CIs, of each reporting element among empirical LLM-evaluation papers in the strata actually coded (Springer OA IS journals; ACL/EMNLP/NAACL industry tracks 2024–2026; ICIS/ECIS if read).
- For each headline claim with reported n and accuracies, whether the reported difference exceeds the interval implied by that n under three stated uncertainty models, and the resulting survival interval (paired lower bound to unpaired upper bound) per model and per stratum.
- Descriptive differences between the IS and industry-NLP strata and over 2023/24–2026, with intervals; and the share of papers whose reporting is too thin to permit RQ2 at all (a result in its own right).
- A reusable codebook and a small tool, so the audit can be repeated on EMNLP 2026 industry and 2027 IS volumes.

**Cannot claim (or must be scoped):**
- Anything about **IS as a whole**. The codable IS stratum is defined by access: three Springer journals with CC licences, plus ICIS/ECIS only if the author obtains eLibrary access. DSS, ISR, I&M, JMIS, MISQ, EJIS, ISJ and JAIS can be *counted* (the Crossref figures above) but not coded from here. The paper must say "IS journals with open full text" and list the excluded venues with their hit counts.
- A **claim survival point estimate**. Without item-level results the paired variance is unknown; report bounds.
- **Causes** of poor reporting (page limits, reviewer norms) — the design is observational on papers.
- **Representativeness over time.** 2023 has 2 BISE, 1 ISF, 2 EM hits; the IS time series effectively starts in 2025 (the 2026 counts dominate: BISE 12, ISF 28, EM 11, DSS 16, ISR 27). RQ3's temporal component is really 2025 vs 2026 for IS and 2024–2026 for industry tracks.
- **Precise venue contrasts**: with ~40 vs ~150 papers, only differences of roughly 15–20 points are resolvable.
- That IS is worse than CS in absolute terms: the comparators (Angermeir et al. on reproducibility; Bean et al. on construct validity; "Awes, Laws, and Flaws" on statistical tests) measure different constructs on different frames; the comparison is qualitative unless the codebook adopts their item definitions verbatim.

## 6. Blockers

1. **AIS eLibrary full texts are gated** (the ICIS 2025 PDF request redirected to `login.cgi?...situation=subscription`). Without AIS membership or a subscribing library, the ICIS/ECIS stratum (171 title hits) is abstract-only and must be dropped or reduced to abstract-level coding of headline claims. This is the single largest frame restriction and changes the IS stratum from ~80–120 to ~30–50 papers.
2. **Second coder not yet recruited.** Kappa is non-negotiable for a reporting-practice audit aimed at BISE/ISJ/JAIS; budget 20–55 h of a second person's time.
3. **Crossref abstracts are missing for Elsevier, Taylor & Francis and (partly) Springer**, so the title+abstract counts undercount; the final Springer frame should be built from Springer's own search or from OpenAlex when its rate limit allows, and reconciled with Crossref.
4. **Paired variance is unidentifiable from published tables**; the design must be amended to report survival bounds (section 2). Design correction, not a resource issue.
5. **`bayes_evals` is not on PyPI and depends on pandas**; either add the repository to the session and install pandas/numpy, or reimplement the Beta-Binomial interval in the standard library (small).
6. **Undermining-evidence check is incomplete.** The six searches found no IS-venue audit of statistical or judge-validation practice, but they did surface "Awes, Laws, and Flaws" (2,000+ LLM papers, statistical-test criterion) and a 12-paper agent-benchmark disclosure audit (2605.21404), both of which must be positioned against; the ISJ methodological-guidance paper (10.1111/isj.70027) was seen only by title. The WebSearch budget ended before an aisel.aisnet.org query for "reporting" + "reproducibility" + "large language model" could be run beyond the first (which returned only application papers).
7. **The frame has moved since the idea record**: ACL 2026 industry (154 papers) exists and should be added; EMNLP 2026 industry does not yet exist; 2026 IS volumes are still filling (the Springer 2026 counts will grow through the year), so the sampling date must be frozen and stated.

None is a hard stop; 1 and 2 are modest external needs, 3–5 and 7 are design/setup, 6 is reading.

## 7. Verdict

**feasible-now** (for the design restricted to Springer OA IS journals plus ACL/EMNLP/NAACL industry tracks); the as-written design including ICIS/ECIS full texts and inter-coder reliability is **needs-external-resources**, but the resources are small (see section 8).

Reasoning: the sampling frame is enumerable today from public APIs (Crossref by ISSN gave 3,583 IS journal articles with 83 term hits in the three Springer journals, 65 of them CC-licensed; the AIS track crawl gave 1,557 ICIS/ECIS papers with 171 title hits; the ACL Anthology gave 503 industry-track papers in the four named volumes and 154 more in ACL 2026). Full-text access is confirmed for the two strata that carry the confirmatory hypotheses: a Springer CC BY PDF downloaded as `application/pdf` and an ACL industry PDF answered 200 `application/pdf`. The method is human coding plus closed-form statistics; it needs no LLM, no GPU and no paid API, so scenarios A and B are equivalent, and the only real cost is 150–220 person-hours for a 150-paper audit. The baselines exist and are reproducible (Miller's formulae are closed-form; `bayes_evals` is MIT-licensed on GitHub with a documented API; `evalci` exists on PyPI but is immature). No approvals are needed. The claims are descriptive with intervals and must be scoped to "IS journals with open full text", with the paired-variance bound and the small-n venue comparison stated explicitly. What the restricted design loses is the ICIS/ECIS stratum, which is where most IS empirical LLM evaluations appear to sit (76 title hits in ICIS 2025 alone); that loss is the reason the full design is rated needs-external-resources rather than feasible-now.

## 8. Minimum change in resources that would unblock the full design

Two small additions: (1) **AIS eLibrary access** — an individual AIS membership or any subscribing library login — so the author can download the ~171 ICIS/ECIS candidate PDFs manually (8–15 h), restoring the stratum that holds most IS empirical LLM evaluations and roughly doubling the IS n; and (2) **one second coder for 25–55 hours** to code a 25 % reliability sample, which makes the kappa reporting credible for BISE/ISJ/JAIS. Neither needs money beyond a membership fee, compute, or approvals. Everything else (Springer OA PDFs, ACL industry PDFs, the Crossref/ACL frame, the re-analysis tool) is available on the present machine today. If Elsevier/INFORMS access were also obtained (DSS 25, ISR 34, I&M 12 title hits), the IS stratum would become representative of the field's main journals rather than of its open-access subset; that is desirable but not necessary for the confirmatory hypotheses.
