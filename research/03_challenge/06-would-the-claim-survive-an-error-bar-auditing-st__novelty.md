# Novelty challenge: "Would the Claim Survive an Error Bar? Auditing Statistical and Judge-Validation Practice in Published LLM Evaluations in IS and Industry-Track Venues"

Reviewer stance: adversarial (task is to refute novelty). Date of search: 2026-10-05. Idea source: `research/02_candidate_ideas_merged.json`, `ideas[5]`.

Idea in one sentence: code 2023-2026 empirical LLM-evaluation papers in IS journals (BISE, ISF, Electronic Markets; ICIS/ECIS where PDFs can be obtained) and ACL/EMNLP/NAACL industry tracks for item counts, repeated runs, decoding settings, model version, uncertainty reporting and (for LLM judges) human validation with a chance-corrected statistic (RQ1); then re-analyse each paper's headline comparison against the interval implied by its own n under a naive binomial SE, Miller-style clustered SE with an ICC range, and a Bowyer-style Beta-Binomial interval, yielding a "claim survival rate" (RQ2); compare IS against industry-NLP venues over time (RQ3); release the codebook and a standard-library survival calculator.

## 3. Verdict: partially-covered

Reasoning (why not "likely novel"):

1. The claim-level re-analysis construct is already published in a neighbouring field. Christodoulou et al. (2025) take a cohort of medical-imaging papers and compute, from each paper's reported results, a Bayesian probability that the claimed ranking "is likely to have occurred by chance", reporting that 86% of classification papers carry a false-claim probability above 5%. That is the idea's "claim survival rate" with the sign flipped. Oh (2026) does a leaderboard-level version of the same audit for AI benchmarks, Mandujano Reyes (2026) and evalci (2026) re-analyse MMLU/MMLU-Pro rankings with Miller's clustered machinery and multiple-comparison correction, and Card et al. (2020) ran the power-based version on GLUE-era NLP. The idea's text frames the retrospective application of Miller and Bowyer et al. as the gap; the gap is narrower than stated: what has not been done is applying three uncertainty models to the headline claims of IS and industry-track LLM papers.
2. The reporting-prevalence audit (RQ1) is a crowded genre with LLM-specific instances since 2025. Bean et al. (NeurIPS 2025) code 445 LLM benchmark papers and report 16.0% with any statistical testing and 17.1% using LLM judges; Yang et al. (2026) code 3,334 NLG papers and report human validation of LLM judges in fewer than 8%; Siddiq et al. (2025) annotate 640 LLM-for-SE papers for versioning and model smells with venue effects; Marie et al. (2021), Dror et al. (2018) and Groß (2020) did venue-by-year statistical-practice audits before LLMs, Groß with a psychology comparator and venue regression that matches the idea's RQ3 design. The idea's H2 (fewer than 25% of judge papers report chance-corrected agreement) is already close to settled by Yang et al.'s under-8% human-validation figure for NLP main tracks; a reviewer will ask what the IS and industry-track numbers add beyond a replication in a smaller sample.
3. The checklist deliverable exists several times over. Baltes et al. (EMSE, 22 authors) already publish a living checklist that includes model-version reporting and human validation of LLM outputs; Ulmer et al. (2022), Yang et al. (2026), Rao and Callison-Burch (2026, judge agreement), Norman et al. (2026, Minimum Viable Validation Protocol), EvalCards, Eval Factsheets and Evaluation Cards cover the rest. An IS-editor checklist derived from the audit would be a localisation, not a new instrument; Jinnath and Zhao (2026) further show that checklist compliance at EMNLP 2025 is often surface-level, which argues for the idea's direct-coding approach but also against presenting a checklist as the contribution.
4. Contradicting or complicating evidence the idea must address. Rao and Callison-Burch show that the same judge verdicts can yield kappa on either side of zero depending on protocol choices, so coding "reports a chance-corrected statistic" as a binary quality marker is itself fragile; the codebook needs protocol fields (scale, abstention handling, pooling). Saidi et al. (Patterns 2025) show an inverse accuracy-sample-size relation in published ML, so small-n IS papers may show inflated differences for reasons (publication bias, overfitting) that the sampling-only survival models would misattribute to noise alone. AlHamidi et al. (2026) show that execution-level variance can exceed within-run sampling intervals, so a claim that "survives" the Beta-Binomial interval may still not replicate; the survival rate should be presented as an upper bound on robustness.
5. On the first-check items in the idea's undermining_evidence: no IS-venue meta-research on reporting, error bars, repeated runs or judge validation in LLM papers surfaced in five aisel-filtered WebSearch queries (filters leaked; the only aisel hits were applied papers), in Crossref container-title-filtered searches of seven IS journals, or in the 2025-2026 IS editorials and commentaries found (ISJ 10.1111/isj.70027; ACM TMIS 10.1145/3721846), all of which are guidance rather than audits. The direct AIS eLibrary search was blocked (HTTP 403), so this is an absence within limits, not a confirmed absence. Bean et al. do not stratify by venue per the HTML Q&A pass, so that weakening does not apply.

What was not found after 25 WebSearch, 6 arXiv API, 5 Crossref and 8 blocked dblp/Semantic Scholar/AIS attempts, and therefore remains the idea's claimable contribution: no audit of LLM-evaluation papers in IS journals or conferences, and none of ACL/EMNLP/NAACL industry tracks specifically; no study that pairs reporting-prevalence coding with a per-claim survival re-analysis under three uncertainty models (binomial, clustered with ICC sensitivity, Beta-Binomial) on the paper's own reported n; no study that codes judge validation as a measurement-validity component alongside statistical practice and venue; and no IS versus industry-NLP comparison over 2023-2026. These are field-transfer and design-combination increments on constructs that Christodoulou et al., Bean et al., Yang et al., Siddiq et al. and Groß have each already published in neighbouring literatures; "partially-covered" is the appropriate verdict.

Recommendations if the idea proceeds: cite Christodoulou et al. (2025) as the direct antecedent of the survival re-analysis and frame RQ2 as a transfer of their design to LLM evaluation with sampling-model sensitivity; use Bean et al.'s 16.0% and Yang et al.'s under-8% as pre-registered comparators for H1 and H2 and state the expected IS-stratum CI width (50-80 papers) before coding; add protocol fields to the judge codes following Rao and Callison-Burch; adopt Groß's venue-by-year regression and psychology comparator as the RQ3 template; drop the checklist from the contribution list or position it as an IS adaptation of Baltes et al.'s living guidelines; read in full Christodoulou et al., Siddiq et al. (venue effects), Bean et al. (codebook) and Yang et al. before committing, since this review saw abstracts and one question-and-answer pass only.
