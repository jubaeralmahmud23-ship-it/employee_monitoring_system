## 2. Labels and measurements: are they adequate for the intended claim?

- **True quality (the "signal").** Code: execution-verified pass/fail from HumanEvalPack's buggy solutions (shipped), from mutated MBPP variants (constructed, labelled by running tests) and optionally from CodeJudgeBench (shipped, 5,352 pairs). This is a hard binary label and the cleanest arm. Customer support: WixQA ships only expert answers; the low-quality counterpart is a construction (deleted steps, wrong facts, generic text). The resulting label is "degraded by design", which is adequate for a controlled experiment but is not a human rating of quality, and the degradation severity becomes a hidden nuisance factor that must be fixed by a rule and reported. Open-ended: MT-bench human votes and LLMBar gold labels give relative quality only, so the absolute-rubric arm cannot be scored against ground truth there; it can only be scored for rank reversals within a pair.
- **Cue manipulation (the "gaming").** Cues are the study's own text (assertive self-assessment, seniority claim, prior-rating anchor, note-to-reviewer instruction, politeness/effort signal). They are assigned by design to artefacts, so the cue effect is experimentally identified. Two measurement decisions are required before pre-registration: where the cue is placed (prefix, suffix, inline comment; "Bias in the Loop" attaches cues before the candidate; the grading paper inserts student answers mid-prompt) and whether the cue is counted toward length (a verbosity confound, which the mitigation "metadata stripping" also removes).
- **Score shift and gaming-to-signal ratio.** Computable from the absolute 1-10 scores: mean(score | cue, quality) minus mean(score | no cue, quality), divided by mean(score | high) minus mean(score | low) without cue. Reading the next-token distribution over the digit tokens (llama-cpp-python exposes logprobs) gives an expected score per call and avoids parse failures; this is cheaper and more stable than sampling justifications.
- **Rank-reversal rate (H3).** For each (high, low) pair and cue, a reversal is score(low+cue) > score(high). With 300 pairs per cue and bootstrap or Beta-binomial intervals, the pre-registered 10% threshold is testable with a confidence interval of roughly plus or minus 3-4 points at n = 300.
- **Position consistency under swap.** Pairwise calls in both orders; the metric is standard (Zheng et al.; LLMBar; "Bias in the Loop" uses the same orig/swap procedure).
- **Chance-corrected agreement.** Cohen's kappa against pass/fail or human votes, with clustered bootstrap over tasks; `krippendorff` 0.8.2 and statsmodels are on PyPI.
- **Repeated runs.** llama.cpp at temperature 0 with a fixed thread count is deterministic, so "3 runs per cell" only measures something if runs use seeded sampling (temperature > 0) or prompt paraphrases. This must be stated in the plan; Atil et al. style run variance then becomes a nuisance estimate, not a free replication.
- **Adequacy verdict.** Labels are adequate for the code arm and for the pairwise open-ended arm; the support arm rests on construction labels; absolute scores in the open-ended arm have no ground truth. The intended claim (cue effects relative to quality sensitivity for small judges) is measurable on the first two arms.

## 5. What the design can and cannot claim

**Can claim (supportable).** Because cues are assigned by design within artefacts of known quality, the study identifies the causal effect of adding a cue text on the ratings of these five judges under these prompts, formats and mitigations, with effect sizes relative to the judges' quality sensitivity, rank-reversal rates and swap consistency. H1 (instructions and prior-rating anchors raise low-quality scores), H2 (absolute scoring more gameable than reference-guided pairwise) and H3 (rank reversals above 10% under some cue for some model) are all directly testable with pre-specified estimands and intervals. A null result still yields the audit protocol and a robustness floor, as the idea says.

**Cannot claim.** (1) Anything about real worker behaviour or real HR artefacts: the cues are stylised and the artefacts are benchmark items; "worker" is a framing, not a population. (2) Anything about frontier API judges or vendor products: only 1-4B open models are tested, and the grading paper reports that Claude-Sonnet-4.6 resisted two of three attacks while Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B did not, so small-judge results do not transfer upward. (3) Absolute-score validity in the open-ended arm (no ground truth there). (4) Quality-of-work claims beyond pass/fail and constructed degradations. (5) Firm claims from HumanEval alone, given contamination; MBPP, CodeJudgeBench and WixQA must carry the inference. (6) Novelty claims of the "first" kind: Section 6 lists prior work that already shows (a) absolute scoring is more attackable than comparative scoring (Raina, Liusie and Gales, EMNLP 2024, abstract), (b) assertive comments, self-justifying notes, authority and provenance cues shift Qwen3-4B and Qwen2.5-Coder-3B code verdicts ("Bias in the Loop", 2026, full text), (c) heuristic "give me full credit" injections inflate rubric grades on Qwen3-4B with average ASR 56.9% across five graders and preventive instructions leave ASR above 30% for all but Claude (arXiv 2606.03090, full text), and (d) seniority and authorship labels shift LLM reviews (arXiv 2509.13400, Findings of ACL 2026, abstract; arXiv 2608.18091, EMNLP Findings 2026, abstract). The defensible contribution is the worker-controllable cue taxonomy, the gaming-to-signal ratio and rank-reversal estimands, the mitigation comparison for on-premise 1-4B judges across code, support and open-ended artefacts, and the IS framing; H2 is a replication on small models rather than a new finding.

## 6. Blockers

1. **Compute time, not capability.** Every cell runs on CPU, but the sampled design is 250-500 wall-clock hours and must be scheduled as a background job over two to three weeks; the exhaustive factorial is impossible here. The pilot must come first to fix real throughput.
2. **Construction labels for the support arm.** WixQA ships no low-quality answers; degraded variants are labelled by construction, and no human validation is available (no participants). This limits the support arm to "degraded by rule" claims.
3. **Novelty erosion, found today (relevant to the claim, not to feasibility).** arXiv 2604.16790 "Bias in the Loop" (April 2026, preprint) already isolates presentation cues (sentiment "definitely handles edge cases", verbosity "self-justifying note", authority, bandwagon, model-name, refined, self-enhance) one at a time on Qwen3-4B and Qwen2.5-Coder-3B over CodeJudgeBench with orig/swap pairs; arXiv 2606.03090 (June 2026, preprint) tests heuristic, agentic and many-shot injections against rubric graders including Qwen3-4B and evaluates preventive-instruction and guard-model defenses; arXiv 2505.13348 (May 2025, preprint) runs GCG suffix attacks on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Falcon3-3B judges over MT-bench human judgments (CUA ASR above 30%); arXiv 2506.09443 RobustJudge (published version in IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security 2026 per Crossref, DOI 10.1109/tifs.2026.3737714) covers 15 attacks and 8 defenses on 13 judges of 7B and above; five 2025-2026 journal papers on grading injection exist (EvalHack, Information 2026, DOI 10.3390/info17030297; SteadyEval, Computers 2026, DOI 10.3390/computers15010055; "When AI Is Fooled", Education Sciences 2025, DOI 10.3390/educsci15111419; Cai, Scientific Reports 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-46563-1; Humble, Journal of Academic Ethics 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10805-026-09758-9; all metadata only). The two 2026 titles the idea flagged as unknown are now resolved and do not overlap: AMEL (arXiv 2605.22714) studies conversation-history polarity effects, and "Jagged Judges" (arXiv 2608.12645) studies pushback and persuasion in multi-turn judging; neither embeds cues in the rated artefact.
4. **Repository dependencies.** LLMBar data lives on GitHub (the HF loader script cannot run under `datasets` 5.0.1); bayes_evals, Open-Prompt-Injection and the paired-buggy-code repository are GitHub-only. Each must be added to the session before use; none is strictly essential.
5. **Semantic Scholar unavailable today** (429 on every call): bibliographic checks rely on Crossref and arXiv; this affects the write-up, not the experiment.

## 7. Verdict

**feasible-but-slow-on-cpu.** All data exist, are ungated and are licensed for research use; the code arm ships execution-verified correct/incorrect pairs; all five judges have ungated weights and GGUF quantisations; llama-cpp-python compiles here; no participants or approvals are needed; the within-artefact factorial supports a causal claim about cue effects on these judges. The only hard constraint is throughput: a pilot of about 2,200 calls fits in a day, a minimal confirmatory set of about 20,000 calls takes 3.5-6 days of continuous CPU, and the pre-registered sampled design of 60,000-100,000 calls takes two to three weeks. The claim remains supportable at the trimmed size if the mitigation arm is restricted to the strongest cues and three judges, and if the paper's contribution is positioned against the 2026 code-judge and grading-injection studies listed above rather than as a first demonstration.

## 8. Minimum change in resources that would unblock the full design

Any one of: (a) a single consumer GPU with 8-16 GB VRAM (or a shared GPU hour budget of about 30-40 hours), which turns 450-500 CPU hours into roughly 20-40 hours and makes the full sampled factorial plus prompt-variant replication practical; (b) a second 4-core machine or a 16-core node for about two weeks, halving or quartering wall-clock time with no code change (judgments are embarrassingly parallel across models and cells); (c) without new hardware, cut the design to three judges (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B, Phi-4-mini), two formats (absolute, pairwise with swap) and two mitigations on the two strongest cues, about 30,000 calls, which fits in about one week of background CPU and still tests H1-H3. Nothing else (data, licences, approvals, software) needs to change.
