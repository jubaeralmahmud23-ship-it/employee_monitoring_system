# Novelty challenge: "Do Language Models Read the Log or the Labels? A Label-Intervention Protocol for Semantics-Aware Next-Activity Prediction at Varying Training Sizes"

Reviewer stance: adversarial (task is to refute novelty). Date of search: 2026-10-03. Idea source: `research/02_candidate_ideas_merged.json`, `ideas[0]`.

Idea in one sentence: cross public 4TU logs x training-set size (50 to full) x activity-label condition (original, anonymised codes, synonym paraphrase, meaning-conflicting relabeling, reversed stage names) x predictor family (argmax / Markov / one-hot LSTM as label-invariant baselines; sentence-embedding classifiers; 1.5-4B open LLMs; LoRA-tuned 1.5B), and report (a) semantic dependence = acc(original) - acc(anonymised), (b) an evidence-fidelity rate on a constructed conflict set, (c) learning curves with trace-clustered intervals.

## 3. Verdict: partially-covered

Reasoning (why not "likely novel"):

1. The two measurement constructs are not new. "Semantic dependence via anonymised labels" is exactly Padella, de Leoni and Dumas's RQ2 (semantic hashing of all context-sensitive strings "to isolate semantic understanding from distributional patterns", with the observation that classical learners are unaffected by it), already run in a 100-trace data-scarce PPM setting. "Evidence fidelity under reversed/shuffled process evidence" is Kourani et al.'s construct and manipulation. The idea's own text acknowledges both, but the working title and contribution (1) present the protocol as new; a reviewer will read it as a recombination.
2. The protocol logic exists outside BPM under other names. Wei et al. (2023) define the flipped-label and semantically-unrelated-label setups that correspond one-to-one to the idea's "meaning-conflicting relabeling" and "anonymised codes" arms, and already report the scale-dependence that the idea's H3 predicts; Windisch et al. (JMIR AI 2026) run progressive content removal plus counterfactual testing of evidence use versus identifier-conditioned prior knowledge; TabLLM's column-name ablations (per snippet) make the same point for tabular few-shot learning. The idea should be framed explicitly as a port of these designs to log-grounded sequence prediction, and the related-work section must cite Wei et al.
3. The baseline and model space is already occupied. Weytjens and Weber (BPM 2026 Forum) supply argmax parity on the same seven logs with the same metrics; Fertig et al. (ECML PKDD 2026 workshops) already LoRA-tune 1-2B open LLMs (Llama-3.2-1B, Gemma-2-2b) against a Majority baseline on BPI20 logs and find LLMs lagging; Bollig et al. show n-gram parity. Reproducing these again at full training size adds little; the value is only in the crossing with label conditions and training sizes.
4. Contradicting motivation to address. Abb et al. (2023) and Pfeiffer et al. (BISE 2025) show that leakage in the standard public logs makes trivial predictors strong, which means a null semantic gain at n >= 1000 (H2) could be a leakage artefact rather than a finding about semantics; Tan et al. (NeurIPS 2024) show pretrained LMs do not help few-shot numeric forecasting. The design needs a leakage-controlled test set (e.g. prefixes unseen in training, or the scenario construction of Pfeiffer et al.) before H2 can be interpreted.

What was not found after 31 queries, and therefore remains the idea's claimable contribution: no next-activity-prediction study that (a) crosses label interventions, including meaning-conflicting and reversed-stage relabeling, with (b) a training-size axis from 50 traces to full and (c) label-invariant baselines on public 4TU logs, nor (d) any conflict-set construction (prefixes where log-majority next activity and label-implied next activity differ) yielding a fidelity rate for predictive monitoring. The learning-curve tables with open CPU-runnable models would also be new relative to Padella et al.'s single proprietary model. These are protocol-level and empirical increments, not a new construct; "partially-covered" is the appropriate verdict.

Recommendations if the idea proceeds: rename the contribution from "a label-intervention protocol" to "a label-intervention replication and extension" citing Padella et al., Kourani et al. and Wei et al. as the three antecedents; add a leakage-controlled test split; pre-register the conflict-set construction and its base rate; treat Fertig et al.'s LoRA results as the prior for the LLM arm; read the full texts of Padella et al. (hashing section 5.3.2), Fertig et al. and Windisch et al. before committing, since this review only grepped or saw snippets of them.
