# Novelty challenge: "Does Quantization Hurt Bangla More? A CPU-Only Factorial of Precision, Model Size and Script for Generative and Retrieval Models, with a Cost-Matched Lexical-versus-Dense Frontier for Bengali, Swahili and Indonesian"

Reviewer stance: adversarial (task is to refute novelty). Date of search: 2026-10-05. Idea source: `research/02_candidate_ideas_merged.json`, `ideas[7]` (merged raw ideas 10 and 11).

Idea in one sentence: Part A runs 1-4B instruction-tuned decoders (Qwen3, Qwen2.5, gemma-3-4b, Llama-3.2-3B, TigerLLM-1B, sarvam-1) through llama.cpp on a 4-core CPU at bf16, Q8_0, Q6_K, Q4_K_M, Q3_K_M and Q2_K on parallel Belebele / Global-MMLU items plus per-language classification, extractive QA and summarisation sets for bn, sw, id and en, with tokenizer fertility folded into cost per item, and tests a language x precision interaction (H1), a size x precision interaction (H2) and a fertility-adjusted cost gap (H3); Part B builds a cost-matched retrieval frontier (nDCG@10 and Recall@100 per CPU-second, per GB RAM, per index byte) on MIRACL / Mr. TyDi bn, sw, id with whitespace and tokenization-aware BM25, multilingual-e5-small/base and MiniLM in fp32 and ONNX int8, bge-m3 as an upper bound, RRF and linear hybrids, and five nested corpus tiers, testing whether tokenization-aware BM25 helps Bangla more (H4), whether int8 encoder loss is larger for Bangla (H5) and whether the hybrid margin shrinks with corpus size (H6); RQ3 compares the sign and size of the script x precision interaction across decoders (H1) and encoders (H5).

## 3. Verdict: partially-covered (joint); Part A partially-covered, leaning largely-covered on the construct; Part B partially-covered

### 3a. Part A (generative quantization-by-script factorial): partially-covered

1. The core construct (language x precision interaction with non-Latin scripts suffering more, at small sizes, on parallel MCQ items) is already published or in preprint three times over in 2026 at the idea's model sizes. Thomas (2608.11786) reports 2-4x larger INT3 GPTQ perplexity harm for non-Latin than Latin scripts on Qwen2.5-3B and Llama-3.2-3B with GlobalMMLU accuracy, exactly two of the idea's planned checkpoints and one of its planned benchmarks. Soualhi (2608.09941, under review at EMNLP 2026) runs 4-bit nf4 on Gemma 4 2B/4B and Qwen 3.5 2B/4B across eight languages including Swahili on MMLU Pro X Lite and Global PIQA and frames the result as "typological fragility". Marie and Fujita (2508.20893) already include Qwen3-1.7B with GGUF Q4_K_M and Q2_K and Bengali. A reviewer will treat the idea's H1 and H2 as replications of these three, and the idea's "no record received crosses quantizer level down to 2-4 bits [and] model size in the CPU-deployable 1-4B range" is no longer accurate (the idea text predates the August 2026 preprints).
2. Check (a) weakens H1 for GGUF specifically. In Marie and Fujita's Qwen3-1.7B rows, GGUF Q4_K_M costs Bengali 1.6 COMET versus 0.7 for Japanese and essentially nothing for French and Malayalam, i.e. a sub-two-point difference at the idea's main deployment level; at Q2_K the Bengali drop (19.3 into, 18.8 out of English) is large, but Latin-script Polish loses 16.5 in one direction and Latin-script Zulu loses nothing, and the authors read the pattern as an "inverse-performance law" (worse baseline, larger loss), not a script law. Soualhi's table likewise shows Swahili (Latin) losing more than English in both Gemma sizes. So the pre-existing per-language small-model GGUF evidence is consistent with "baseline competence, not script, predicts loss", which is the alternative explanation the idea's H1 must rule out and does not currently model (the mixed-effects design has no baseline-accuracy covariate).
3. The GGUF ladder at small sizes is covered in English. Davidsson and Nugues (2605.19645) run eight LLMs through Q2_K to Q8_0 on MMLU-Pro, CRUXEval and MuSR and already report that larger models are more resilient, i.e. H2 in English; Hossain et al. cover Bangla at 8-bit for 7B-20B and name INT4/Q4_K_M and a family x format x size grid as their own next step, which signals that the same group may fill the cell first.
4. What survives as Part A's claimable contribution after 55 queries: no study was found that (i) uses GGUF/llama.cpp k-quants at five levels on 1-4B instruction models, (ii) on Bengali, Swahili and Indonesian together with English on parallel items, (iii) across classification, reading comprehension, extractive QA and summarisation rather than MCQ or MT alone, (iv) with measured CPU throughput and RAM on a fixed 4-core budget and fertility-adjusted cost per item, and (v) with a pre-registered interaction test and a baseline-score covariate. Items (iv) and (v) are the only parts no neighbour has; (i)-(iii) are combinations of existing axes. H3 (fertility-adjusted Bangla cost at least twice English) is close to a foregone conclusion given Roy et al.'s 4.5x Qwen2.5 fertility and Bhowmik et al.'s 5x tokens per word, and should be reframed as a measured cost table, not a hypothesis.

### 3b. Part B (cost-matched lexical-versus-dense frontier): partially-covered

1. The "simple wins at scale" design is English-only in Wang et al. (2607.26497) and the idea's transfer to bn/sw/id with human qrels is a genuine gap: no MIRACL or Mr. TyDi follow-up with CPU-second, RAM or index-byte costs across corpus tiers surfaced in 15 retrieval-focused queries. Kamalloo et al. (ACL 2023 industry) is the closest cost-aware statement for bn and sw, but in API dollars with closed models.
2. However, the infrastructure the idea proposes as a contribution partly exists. HAKARI-Bench (2606.22778) already compares BM25, dense, sparse, late-interaction and rerankers with int8/binary embedding quantization and Matryoshka truncation across 43 languages and 55 models under unified conditions, including NanoMIRACL, and publishes a leaderboard; Bekko Embedding (2607.25180) already reports CPU speed (x86, Raspberry Pi 5) against multilingual-e5-small and ships int8 ONNX builds; Granite Embedding R2 (2605.13521) and Cirillo et al. (2605.23618) report CPU-latency Pareto positions; MILCO (ICLR 2026) adds a learned-sparse arm with latency and index-size figures on MIRACL that the idea's factor list omits. The "released configurations and public results table" in contribution (4) therefore competes with a live leaderboard.
3. H5 (int8 encoder loss larger for Bangla) has no direct precedent, which is in the idea's favour, but the closest study cuts the other way on importance: Han (2609.16391) maps weight-only PTQ of BGE-M3, E5 and others down to INT2 and finds the embedding table is not the fragile part, while explicitly not exercising multilingual retrieval; HAKARI-Bench's appendix heading describes int8/binary output quantization costs as "mild, uniform, and model-specific". If int8 ONNX inference turns out to be near-lossless for all scripts (the idea's own undermining scenario), H5 is a null and the paper is left with the frontier only.
4. Check (c): Saragih et al. (ICADEIS 2026) is confirmed to exist with DOI 10.1109/icadeis71120.2026.11644578 but its abstract was not obtainable; the Indonesian hybrid-fusion cell may already be reported (without cost). The idea's rule "read it first and drop id from Part B if it covers cost and quantization" cannot yet be executed.
5. H4 (tokenization-aware BM25 helps Bangla more than Indonesian) could not be checked against the Anserini/MIRACL analyzer literature because the WebSearch budget expired on that query (#32) and the arXiv API was rate-limited (#38); this remains an open search limit rather than a finding.

### 3c. Joint verdict: partially-covered

RQ3 (same-sign script x precision interaction in decoders and encoders) has no precedent in any result received and is the merged idea's most distinctive element; nothing in the 55 queries tests quantization harm by language in both model classes on the same languages. But it is a comparison of two effect sizes whose first component is now a replication of three 2026 preprints and whose second component may be null. The honest framing is: a CPU-costed, pre-registered replication and extension of Thomas, Soualhi and Marie-Fujita to GGUF k-quants, Bengali/Swahili/Indonesian and non-MCQ tasks, paired with the first (within these search limits) cost-matched lexical-dense-hybrid frontier for those languages on human-judged qrels, with the decoder-encoder comparison as the headline exploratory result. "Partially-covered" is appropriate; "likely-novel" is not defensible for either part on its own, and Part A's construct is close to "largely-covered".

Recommendations if the idea proceeds: cite Thomas (2608.11786), Soualhi (2608.09941) and Davidsson-Nugues (2605.19645) as direct antecedents and rewrite the problem statement, which currently says no 2-4-bit 1-4B cross-script record exists; add baseline (bf16) accuracy as a covariate so that H1 tests script after controlling for the "inverse-performance law"; drop H3 as a hypothesis and report fertility-adjusted cost descriptively; re-read Bhowmik et al. on format-failure asymmetry before relying on exact-match scoring; obtain the Saragih et al. abstract (IEEE Xplore, manually) before fixing the Indonesian arms; add a learned-sparse arm (MILCO or BGE-M3 sparse) and compare the released table against HAKARI-Bench's leaderboard rather than presenting it as the first such table; check Marie and Fujita's HF results dataset for sw and id rows (not read here) since it may already contain the MT version of H1 for all four of the idea's languages.
