### Idea 1. Do Language Models Read the Log or the Labels? A label-intervention protocol for semantics-aware next-activity prediction at varying training sizes

**Lens:** cs-technical (raw idea 1; no merge). **runs_without_llm:** false.

**Problem and RQs.** Weytjens and Weber (BPM 2026 Forum) find a counting argmax baseline matches vocabulary-adapted LLMs on seven 4TU logs but discard label text; Padella, de Leoni and Dumas (arXiv 2601.11468) find Gemini 2.5 Flash winning with 100 traces and attribute part of the gain to label semantics via hashing, with one proprietary model and no trivial baseline; Kourani et al. show knowledge-driven hallucination only for text-to-model generation. RQ1: as a function of training size (50 traces to full), how much next-activity accuracy is attributable to label semantics rather than log statistics, against argmax, Markov and one-hot LSTM baselines? RQ2: when labels are rewritten to conflict with the log, do semantics-aware predictors follow labels or evidence, and does this depend on training size?

**Why it matters.** Predictive process monitoring is being re-tooled around LLMs on the strength of data-scarce wins; organisations with small logs are those for whom a semantic prior could help or mislead. Addresses agents-BPM digest gaps 1-2 (when LLM semantics help; evidence fidelity on log-grounded tasks).

**Contribution.** A measurement protocol (five label conditions with label-agnostic baselines as manipulation checks), learning curves on public logs with trace-clustered intervals, and a conflict-set construction yielding a fidelity rate. A null on semantic gain is itself the answer.

**Design.** Logs (BPI12, BPI17, three BPI20 sub-logs, Sepsis, Road Traffic Fines; English-label logs carry the semantic arms) x training size (seven levels, temporal split as Weytjens and Weber) x label condition (original, anonymised codes, synonym paraphrase, meaning-conflicting permutation, reversed stage names) x predictor family (argmax, Markov, one-hot LSTM; sentence-embedding kNN/logistic; few-shot 1.5-4B GGUF models via llama.cpp; LoRA on the 1.5B at n <= 500). Measures: accuracy, macro-F1, semantic dependence = acc(original) - acc(anonymised), evidence fidelity on the conflict set, ECE where available. Preregistered H1 (baselines invariant within 0.5 points), H2 (semantic dependence declines with n, indistinguishable from zero at n >= 1000), H3 (fidelity of semantics-aware predictors at n <= 100 below argmax, gap closing with n).

**Data resources.** 4TU.ResearchData logs by DOI (all HTTP 200 on 2026-10-03); pm4py; ungated Hugging Face models (Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B-Instruct, Qwen3-1.7B/4B, SmolLM2-1.7B, Phi-3.5-mini, MiniLM and bge-small embeddings). The "Bac" log lives in IBM's GitHub repository; Padella's hospital log is private.

**Risks (card).** Label manipulations can be dismissed as artificial; Dutch labels in BPI12/17; conflict set is a selected subset; small models may be near chance in every condition; CPU few-shot inference is prefill-dominated; LoRA on CPU only for 1.5B at small n.

**Journal audience (card).** Process Science or Information Systems (Elsevier); BPM or ICPM first; DSS as alternative.

**Closest prior art found by the novelty check** (`03_challenge/01-do-language-models-read-the-log-or-the-labels-a-__novelty.md`, Section 2):
- Padella, de Leoni, Dumas, "Exploring LLM Features in Predictive Process Monitoring for Small-Scale Event-Logs", 2026, arXiv preprint (Crossref best match 0.747 is probably the earlier LNBIP 2025 version), https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11468 — RQ2 already anonymises all context-sensitive strings "to isolate semantic understanding from distributional patterns".
- Weytjens, Weber, "David vs. Goliath in Next Activity Prediction", 2026, BPM 2026 Forum, LNBIP 10.1007/978-3-032-37877-4_19, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15868.
- Wei et al., "Larger language models do in-context learning differently", 2023, arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03846 — flipped-label and semantically-unrelated-label setups correspond one-to-one to the conflicting and anonymised arms.
- Kourani, Antonov, Berti, van der Aalst, "Knowledge-Driven Hallucination in Large Language Models", 2025, GenAI4PM 2025 workshop / TechRxiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15336 — source of the evidence-fidelity construct.
- Abb, Pfeiffer, Fettke, Rehse, "A Discussion on Generalization in Next-Activity Prediction", 2023, BPM 2023 workshops, LNBIP 10.1007/978-3-031-50974-2_2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09618 — leakage in public logs makes trivial predictors strong.

**Where the idea still differs** (novelty file, Section 3): no next-activity study crosses label interventions including meaning-conflicting and reversed-stage relabeling with a training-size axis and label-invariant baselines on public 4TU logs, and no conflict-set construction yielding a fidelity rate exists for predictive monitoring; learning curves with open CPU-runnable models would be new relative to Padella's single proprietary model. The file calls these "protocol-level and empirical increments, not a new construct" and recommends renaming the contribution to "a label-intervention replication and extension".

**Data options and blockers from the feasibility check** (`03_challenge/01-...__feasibility.md`): all seven logs reachable via DOI (4TU General Terms of Use; CC BY-NC 4.0 for BPI20 sub-logs); all models ungated with GGUF builds (Phi-3.5 and SmolLM2 only as third-party conversions). Compute (scenario A only): about 735,000 prompts and about 1,400 CPU-hours as written, "Not feasible as written"; trimmed study 250-350 CPU-hours (3-5 weeks); pilot 15-25 CPU-hours. Blockers: LLM arm over budget by an order of magnitude; LoRA on CPU exploratory only; 500 prefixes cannot support a 0.5-point tolerance (paired tests and equivalence margins needed); three GitHub repositories to add; Fertig et al. and van Straten et al. read at abstract level only. Section 8: a 24 GB-class GPU for 30-50 GPU-hours would remove the compute blockers; the local 16 GB card has not been assessed.

**What would undermine it.** Card: a paper with anonymised and conflicting controls plus trivial baselines across training sizes; small models at chance in all conditions; zero semantic gain at n = 50 for embedding classifiers. Check findings: the anonymised-codes construct is *resolved as pre-existing* (Padella RQ2); the fidelity construct is Kourani's; the protocol logic exists outside BPM (Wei et al.; Windisch et al. JMIR AI 2026; TabLLM ablations per snippet). *Open*: full texts of Padella (section 5.3.2), Fertig et al. and van Straten et al. (grepped or abstract only); whether Rebmann et al.'s benchmark contains training-size variation (grep result, not a reading). *New undermining item*: Abb et al. and Pfeiffer et al. (BISE 2025) show example leakage makes a null at large n uninterpretable without a leakage-controlled test split.

### Idea 2. Small Judges, Family Ties: chance-corrected validity, calibration-set requirements, quantisation cost and same-family preference leakage for CPU-runnable 1-4B LLM judges

**Lens:** merged cs-technical evaluation methodology (raw 2) + governance-evaluation validity (raw 8). **runs_without_llm:** false.

**Problem and RQs.** "Reliability without Validity" (Norman, Rivera, Hughes, 2026 preprint) finds exact match overstates Cohen's kappa by 33.8-41.3 points for 21 API judges of 8B and up; Tiwari (2026) uses 7-8B judges with correlation only; Lee et al. (ICML 2026) give a plug-in correction needing a human-labelled calibration set; Preference Leakage (ICLR 2026) shows frontier judges favour related students. RQ1: do quantised 1-4B judges reach usable chance-corrected validity, and what does 4-bit versus 8-bit cost? RQ2: how many calibration items (50-400) bound their error under the Lee et al. correction? RQ3: in a crossed generator-family x judge-family design with objective labels, is there a same-family effect, mediated by perplexity or moderated by size?

**Why it matters.** Projects without API budgets will use small local judges, typically from the generator's family; enterprise RAG and process-mining benchmarks report judge scores without chance-corrected validation (RAG digest gap 1; evaluation digest G1-G2).

**Contribution.** A minimum validation protocol for the no-API regime with quantisation as a factor; a validation table for 1-4B judges; calibration-set size curves; a factorial estimate of the same-family effect with quality fixed by objective labels; released judgments and code.

**Design.** Stage 1: seven judges (Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B, Qwen3-1.7B/4B, Phi-3.5-mini, SmolLM2-1.7B, Granite-3.1-2B) at Q8 and Q4 on MT-bench human votes, LLMBar, JudgeBench and a RewardBench subsample, both orders, with heuristic baselines and the published GPT-4 verdicts; kappa, Krippendorff's alpha, swap consistency, test-retest, Bayesian intervals, Lee et al. correction at 50/100/200/400 calibration items; pass criterion kappa >= 0.4 and position consistency >= 0.8. Stage 2: five generators answer about 300 objectively labelled prompts; every cross-family pair judged by every judge in both orders; mixed-effects logistic regression on same_family. Stage 3 exploratory: perplexity mediator, size scaling, label manipulation, templates. Compute as written about 10,000 pairs x 2 x 3 x 14 cells, cut to 20-40k judgments.

**Data resources.** lmsys/mt_bench_human_judgments (cc-by-4.0), princeton-nlp/LLMBar (MIT; data on GitHub), ScalerLab/JudgeBench (MIT), allenai/reward-bench (odc-by); gated Llama 3.2 and Gemma excluded; bayes_evals, Lee et al. code and Preference-Leakage on GitHub.

**Risks (card).** Contamination; wide intervals on small sets; student labellers in MT-bench; small judges near chance leave the leakage test underpowered; coarse family taxonomy; template confounds; prefill cost on CPU.

**Journal audience (card).** ACL/EMNLP/NAACL main or Findings; GEM workshop; TMLR; BISE or ISF as IS secondary.

**Closest prior art found by the novelty check** (`03_challenge/02-small-judges-family-ties-chance-corrected-validi__novelty.md`, Section 2):
- Awuni et al., "Who Judges Matters: Measuring Family-Conditioned Preference in LLM-as-Judge Panels", 2026 (15 Sep), arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17857 — fully crossed four-family pairwise panel of GGUF Q4_K_M judges on llama.cpp, no API, candidate-family-fixed estimator, log-likelihood mediation (61% attenuation), AB/BA swaps; judges 7-72B.
- Laddha, Pradhan, Srivastava, "SLMJury: Can Small Language Models Judge as Well as Large Ones?", 2026, arXiv preprint with code and leaderboard, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07810 — 16 judges 0.6B-14B including Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B and Qwen3-1.7B/4B, Cohen's kappa among the metrics.
- Yang, Hou, Yang, "When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement", 2026, arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08535 — kappa, position-flip and verbosity bias for Qwen3-1.7B and 4B on LLMBar and three other sets.
- Norman, Rivera, Hughes, "Reliability without Validity" (full title abbreviated here), 2026, arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544 — the protocol; smallest judge 8B.
- Lee, Zeng, Jeong, Sohn, Lee, "How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations", ICML 2026 per arXiv journal_ref, https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21140.

**Where the idea still differs** (Section 3-4 of the novelty file): no paper varies 4-bit versus 8-bit quantisation *of the judge* with chance-corrected human agreement; no calibration-set size curves (50-400) for small open judges; no crossed family design with judges below 7B. The file's recommended repositioning: make quantisation and calibration curves the headline and pre-register Stage 2 as a size-extension of Awuni et al.

**Data options and blockers from the feasibility check** (`03_challenge/02-...__feasibility.md`, scenario A only): all four benchmarks ungated; LLMBar data only on GitHub; official GGUF ladders incomplete, so local quantisation with one pinned llama.cpp build is recommended; Norman et al.'s 541,000 judgments not released. Compute: pilot 5-6 wall-clock hours; trimmed study 250-350 hours (10-15 days); as written 600-750 hours (25-30 days), so the card's "several days" is an underestimate; 32 GB of GGUF versus 29 GB free disk. Blockers: compute, disk, quantisation provenance, four GitHub repositories, the Yang et al. overlap, and prior nulls on self-preference (arXiv 2606.20093; 2608.18091) pointing toward H4 failing. Section 8: an 8-12 GB GPU would run the full design in one or two days; the local card has not been assessed.

**What would undermine it.** Card: kappa/swap/calibration results for <= 4B judges on these sets; Thakur et al. crossing families; Norman et al. including sub-4B models; a published null family effect; Lee et al. shipping small-judge experiments. Check findings: Norman et al. stop at 8B and never mention quantisation (*resolved in the idea's favour*); Lee et al. use API judges only (*resolved*); Thakur et al. include one sub-4B judge and do not cross families (*resolved*). *Newly found and open*: Awuni et al. pre-empt RQ3 at 7-9B; SLMJury and Yang et al. partly pre-empt the validity table; SLMJury's Qwen3-4B at 87.8% on closed-ended items cuts against H3's "near chance" framing; SLMJury's appendix tables and Awuni's "Small judges (7-9B)" row were seen only as headings.

### Idea 3. Gaming the AI Rater: self-presentation cues and embedded instructions in LLM-based evaluation of employee work products

**Lens:** business-IS (raw idea 6; no merge). **runs_without_llm:** false.

**Problem and RQs.** When an organisation lets an LLM rate worker outputs, the rated worker controls the text. RQ1: how much do low-effort cues (assertive self-assessment, seniority claims, prior-rating anchors, a "note to reviewer", politeness) shift ratings from 1-4B judges relative to quality sensitivity? RQ2: how often do they produce rank reversals? RQ3: which cheap mitigations (metadata stripping, rubric anchoring, reference-guided grading, swapped pairwise) reduce gameability and at what cost?

**Why it matters.** Rating is one of Kellogg et al.'s algorithmic-control mechanisms; AI that evaluates workers is high-risk under the EU AI Act; evaluation digest gaps G5-G6 (enterprise-document injection and submitter-side sycophancy with small backbones). Stakeholders: rated workers, HR, vendors, works councils, regulators.

**Contribution.** Effect sizes as a gaming-to-signal ratio with rank-reversal rates; design knowledge on robust scoring formats for on-premise judges; an extension of measurement-gaming and control theory to text-level gaming. Shares judges and harness with idea 2.

### Idea 8. Does Quantization Hurt Bangla More? A CPU-only factorial of precision, model size and script for generative and retrieval models, with a cost-matched lexical-versus-dense frontier for Bengali, Swahili and Indonesian

**Lens:** merged efficiency-low-resource retrieval frontier (raw 10) + quantisation factorial (raw 11). **runs_without_llm:** false.

**Problem and RQs.** Whether post-training quantisation is language-neutral is unresolved: Borgersen and Goodwin find no GGUF effect (70B, English and Norwegian); Marchisio et al. and Marie and Fujita find larger harm for non-Latin scripts and smaller models; Hossain et al. (2026) test Bangla only at 8-bit on 7B-20B models; retrieval "simple wins" results are English-only and uncosted. RQ1: for 1-4B instruction models on 4 CPU cores, how does loss at Q8_0 to Q2_K vary with script, size and task once fertility is in the cost? RQ2: under matched CPU budgets, when does a small embedder (fp32 or int8) or a hybrid beat tokenisation-aware BM25 for bn, sw, id, and is int8 loss larger for Bangla? RQ3: does the script x precision interaction have the same sign in decoders and encoders?

**Why it matters.** A quantised small model plus CPU retrieval is the only private option for an SME or local office in a low-resource-language country (efficiency digest gaps 1, 2, 4).

**Contribution.** A pre-registered factorial on parallel items at bit widths the Bangla study did not reach; a cost-matched retrieval frontier across corpus tiers; a joint test across model classes; released configurations as a selection aid.

**Design.** Part A: eight decoders x six precisions x four languages x tasks (Belebele, Global-MMLU, classification, squad_bn, XL-Sum, legal QA), cost measured; H1 language x precision, H2 size x precision, H3 fertility-adjusted Bangla cost at least twice English. Part B: MIRACL dev (bn 411, sw 482, id 960) and Mr. TyDi; BM25 variants, e5-small/base and MiniLM in fp32 and ONNX int8, bge-m3 upper bound, RRF and linear hybrids, five nested tiers; H4 tokenisation-aware BM25 helps bn more than id, H5 int8 loss larger for bn, H6 hybrid margin shrinks with tier. Reduction plan and companion-paper fallback.

**Data resources.** Belebele (cc-by-sa-4.0), Global-MMLU (apache-2.0), IndoNLU (MIT), MasakhaNEWS (afl-3.0), XL-Sum and squad_bn (cc-by-nc-sa-4.0), Bangla and Swahili sentiment candidates, Bangladesh legal QA (cc-by-4.0), MIRACL and Mr. TyDi (apache-2.0), AgriTrust-RAG (optional); encoders e5, MiniLM, bge-m3; decoders via ungated GGUF mirrors.

**Journal audience (card).** Findings of ACL/EMNLP or the industry track; ECIR reproducibility or SIGIR resource track, IP&M or TOIS; LREC-COLING; ISF or EJISDC for an IS version.

### Recommended 1 (IS-framed primary, for the top-journal goal): Idea 6, the error-bar audit of IS and industry-track LLM evaluations

Reasons tied to the record.
