## 5. Verdict on the integrity of the records

The records are internally consistent at the level that matters for the next decision: every verdict string and rating in the synthesis traces to a dated per-idea file, the three recommended ideas carry the verdicts the recommendation rule requires, no experiment is presented as run, no dataset is marked reachable on a host that refuses fetches, and the area files carry every required section with a 2026-10-03 citation audit and a 2026-10-05 identifier-based reconciliation. The defects found were small and of the kind a second reader catches: two abbreviated paper titles, one author misattribution copied between two files for the same idea, four digest-gap numbers taken from array positions instead of the records' own numbering, two card paragraphs that restated a disproved premise without the correction that appeared two paragraphs later, two scratch timing figures that read as results, one overstated description of a GitHub-hosted CSV, and a resume file whose state table and blockers were two days and one machine out of date; all are now fixed in place. What remains open is reproducibility and verification debt rather than misstatement: the idea-6 frame counts rest on uncommitted scripts, the idea-2 and idea-3 reference-verification tables were never completed, nine citation items await a human decision, and several venue facts and I-O psychology papers are known from third-party snippets or metadata only, all of which the records themselves disclose. The honest summary is that the records describe what was searched, read and measured at the depth actually reached, and that a reader who follows the stated limits will not be misled; the records should not be read as more than a preliminary, abstract-level landscape with adversarial checks, which is what they say they are.
