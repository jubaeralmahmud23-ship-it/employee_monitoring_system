## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Kept:
- Peer-reviewed conference or journal papers whose venue was visible in the result (NeurIPS, AAAI, SIGIR, ACL/EMNLP/NAACL/EACL/TACL, CAIN, KDIR, MAKE, BISE), plus preprints that are central to the enterprise-RAG evaluation debate (GraphRAG, RAG-vs-GraphRAG, unbiased GraphRAG evaluation, EnterpriseRAG, EnterpriseRAG-Bench, Dell deployment reports). Preprints are labelled as such.
- Benchmarks and datasets whose hosting location was visible, to classify GitHub-hosted (reachable) versus HF/Zenodo-hosted (blocked).
- Surveys and literature reviews only as pointers; their claims are not used as findings.

Dropped:
- Vendor, consultancy and platform blogs (atlan, venturebeat, agileinfoways, smarttechlabs, atolio, onyx insights, lushbinary, aivanguard, premai, wz-it, mistral news, IBM community, Snowflake engineering blog, bitdeer, ttms, earley, baytech, usehall, athina, nexocode). Their cost-per-query and "73% of failures are retrieval" style figures are not reported as evidence anywhere in this file.
- Patents (USPTO), student theses (h-da, FH Oberoesterreich, Bologna, Jyvaskyla, webis), secondary aggregator pages (themoonlight, alphaxiv, papers.cool, awesomepapers, sotaverified, paperdigest); aggregators were used only to confirm a paper exists, never as a source of findings.
- Off-scope domains: medical and biomedical RAG benchmarks (RAGCare-QA, biomedical RAG benchmark, GeneRAG, Thyro-GenAI), multimodal RAG (MRAG-Bench, mmRAG), code-generation RAG poisoning, XR interfaces, materials discovery.
- Results whose snippet contained no interpretable finding (e.g. author index pages on aclanthology.org).

Caveat on venue filters: WebSearch's allowed_domains was not honored for aisel.aisnet.org and emerald.com (queries #9 and #23 returned arXiv, patents and biomedical items). The absence of AISeL results therefore says nothing about AISeL coverage.

## Synthesis

### Agreements
- Retrieval, parsing and preprocessing are the dominant bottleneck. This is stated independently by the experience report (Barnett et al.), the practitioner interviews (Brehme et al.), the heterogeneous enterprise benchmark (HERB: retrieval is "the main bottleneck"), the document-analysis benchmark (UDA: parsing and retrieval matter most) and the robustness benchmark (RGB: negative rejection and integration are weak).
- Simple, cheap configurations are stronger than the literature's default framing suggests. DOS RAG matches multi-stage pipelines under matched token budgets; GraphRAG gains shrink under debiased evaluation (Zeng et al.) and reverse on single-hop and time-sensitive questions (Han et al.; GraphRAG-Bench); fusion gains vanish after re-ranking and truncation (Medrano et al.); large LLM bi-encoders add latency for modest gains (Abdallah et al.).
- Evaluation is the weak link. RAGAS/ARES-style judge metrics are the default, but snippets report prompt sensitivity, score inflation and bias; industry still evaluates mostly by hand (Brehme et al.); EKRAG and ARES both try to cut judge cost (embedding/ranking-model judges, fine-tuned small judges).
- For injecting domain knowledge, RAG generally beats domain fine-tuning on the reported metrics (Lakatos et al. and the other items in query #20), with the caveat that combining the two is not straightforward.
- Models do not use long contexts uniformly (Lost in the Middle; BABILong snippet: 10-20% of context used), which is why order-preserving retrieval and routing (Self-Route, DOS RAG) keep appearing.

### Contradictions or tensions
- Graph RAG: Microsoft's report claims substantial improvements on global sensemaking; Han et al. find it helps only on multi-hop/complex reasoning; GraphRAG-Bench (ICLR 2026 snippet) reports 13.4% lower accuracy than vanilla RAG on NQ; Zeng et al. attribute much of the reported gain to evaluation bias. The disagreement is partly about task type and partly about evaluation protocol.
- Hybrid/fusion retrieval: general snippets (StratRAG, TREC notebook) say sparse+dense fusion consistently improves recall, while the Dell deployment finds Hit@10 decreasing after re-ranking and truncation. Offline recall and end-to-end answer availability under a budget are different quantities.
- Noise: Cuconasu et al. find random documents can raise accuracy by up to 35% while near-relevant distractors hurt; RGB finds LLMs struggle with noise and negative rejection; EnterpriseRAG finds a 57-point orchestration gap under noisy retrieval. "Noise" is not defined consistently across these works.
- Long-context vs RAG: Li et al. say LC wins on average when resourced; Laitenberger et al. say a simple retrieve-and-read with LC models is already as good as complex pipelines; Lost in the Middle says LC models degrade mid-context. Which regime an enterprise is in depends on cost constraints that most papers do not report.
- Fine-tuning plus RAG: Lakatos et al. report that combining can decrease performance; another item in query #20 (ACM 10.1145/3703412.3703434, Aggregated Knowledge Model) reports combined models perform best, and a snippet says FT+RAG is best for models up to 3B. Model scale and metric choice appear to drive the difference.
- Faithfulness: several benchmarks centre on faithfulness (RAGTruth, MEMERAG, FaithJudge), while one snippet (query #12) states faithfulness alone is an unreliable quality signal and must be read with answer relevancy.

### Methodological weaknesses
- Synthetic enterprise corpora (HERB, EnterpriseRAG-Bench, DataMorgana) stand in for real internal data; realism of injected noise and cross-document coherence is asserted, not validated against real corpora (inferred from snippets).
- LLM systems are judged by LLM judges; Zeng et al. document question-relatedness and assessment biases, and the Dell judge paper exists because off-the-shelf judges inflate scores. Human-label anchoring (RAGTruth, MEMERAG, EKRAG curation) is rare and small.
- Proprietary evaluations (Dell fusion and judge papers; ScienceDirect industrial KM paper with MRR 88%) are not reproducible; the interview study has n=13.
- Cost, latency and token budget are reported by only a few works (Li et al.; Laitenberger et al.; Abdallah et al.; Medrano et al.); most benchmark papers report accuracy alone.
- Lexical-overlap metrics (ROUGE/BLEU/METEOR in Lakatos et al.; BLEU/ROUGE in CRUD-RAG) remain common despite being poorly suited to generative answers (the Applied Sciences 2026 SLR snippet makes the same point about static hold-out evaluation).
- Multi-turn, unanswerable and instruction-constrained settings are covered by few benchmarks (MTRAG, EnterpriseRAG), and all benchmarks seen are static snapshots; none evaluates a corpus that changes over time.

### Unresolved questions
- Under matched cost and unbiased judging, when does any "advanced" RAG component (graph index, fusion, multi-stage, LLM retriever) pay off on enterprise-style corpora?
- How well do offline retrieval metrics (Recall@k, MRR, nDCG) predict end-to-end answer quality once re-ranking and truncation budgets are fixed? ("Redefining Retrieval Evaluation in the Era of LLMs", arXiv 2510.21440, appeared in results but its findings were not visible.)
- Can small, CPU-runnable judges (fine-tuned classifiers on RAGTruth-style labels, embedding/ranking-model judges as in EKRAG, ARES-style PPI) replace paid LLM judges with acceptable agreement to humans?
- How should RAG over an evolving corpus be validated over time, given Barnett et al.'s claim that validation is only feasible in operation and CRUD-RAG's Update/Delete framing?
- What does the IS/KM literature contribute beyond conceptual pieces (BISE catchword, Emerald chapter) and adoption surveys (KPM 2026)? No empirical IS-journal study of organizational RAG outcomes surfaced in this scan, though the domain filters for AISeL/Emerald were not honored.

## Candidate research gaps

- Budget-matched, cost-reported comparison of simple vs advanced RAG on enterprise-style corpora with open CPU-runnable models. Evidence: DOS RAG, Zeng et al., Han et al., Medrano et al. and Abdallah et al. all show advertised gains shrinking under fair accounting, and none of them uses an enterprise benchmark with cost reported alongside accuracy. Would be disproved by an existing study on EnterpriseRAG-Bench, HERB or UDA that reports accuracy together with token budget, latency and index cost across naive, hybrid, fusion and graph variants.
- Reliability of cheap judges for enterprise QA. Evidence: judge sensitivity and score inflation (queries #16, #41), EKRAG's embedding/ranking-model judges, ARES's fine-tuned small judges, and the practitioner finding that evaluation is mostly human. Would be disproved by published agreement studies showing small open judges match human labels (RAGTruth, MEMERAG, EKRAG) at high agreement on enterprise content.
- Offline-retrieval-to-answer transfer under truncation. Evidence: Dell's Hit@10 drop after re-ranking, Cuconasu et al.'s distractor effect, Lost in the Middle's position effect. Would be disproved by systematic evidence that Recall@k improvements reliably translate into answer-quality improvements across re-ranking depths and context budgets.
- Longitudinal evaluation over an evolving corpus (updates, deletions, conflicting versions, near-duplicates). Evidence: Barnett et al. (validation only in operation; robustness evolves), CRUD-RAG's Update/Delete tasks, EnterpriseRAG-Bench's conflicting-information noise, yet all benchmarks seen are static. Would be disproved by a published benchmark or field study with time-indexed corpus versions and drift measurements.
- Instruction adherence and structured-identifier precision under noisy enterprise retrieval. Evidence: EnterpriseRAG's 80%-per-constraint vs 26.8%-all-constraints gap; Dell's judge paper on error codes/versions; RGB's negative-rejection weakness. Would be disproved by evidence that current models close the orchestration gap once retrieval noise is modelled.
- Empirical IS/KM-theory work on organizational RAG deployment (knowledge quality, governance, adoption, KM outcomes). Evidence: only conceptual (BISE catchword, Emerald chapter) and adoption-survey (KPM 2026) items surfaced; the Applied Sciences 2026 SLR describes a lab-to-market gap. Would be disproved by AISeL/MISQ/JKM empirical studies that this scan missed because the AISeL and Emerald domain filters were not honored.

## Limitations of this scan

- Snippet-only access: no abstract page, PDF or full text was opened; every "finding" above is what a search snippet stated, and numbers (e.g. 32.96, 26.8%, 0.51 to 0.48) are copied from snippets without verification against the papers.
- Search-engine coverage: a single WebSearch backend; allowed_domains was not honored for aisel.aisnet.org and emerald.com, and only partly for sciencedirect.com and dl.acm.org, so IS-journal coverage is unreliable. INFORMS (pubsonline) returned nothing.
- No citation chasing: Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and DBLP were blocked, so forward/backward citation traversal, author disambiguation and venue confirmation were impossible; several author lists are "not shown in snippet", and venue status for some items (e.g. MTRAG) is uncertain.
- Possible snippet errors: one snippet merged EKRAG with EnterpriseRAG-Bench; one gave 1,347 and another 1,348 questions for EKRAG; the search summaries themselves are machine-generated and may paraphrase inaccurately.
- Vendor material was excluded by rule, which also removes practitioner cost data that might be informative; no attempt was made to assess it.
- Date: 2026-10-03; items with 2608/2609 arXiv identifiers are weeks old and may not be peer-reviewed yet.
- This is a non-systematic landscape scan with 47 queries; it is not a systematic review and no screening statistics are implied.
