## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Kept:
- Peer-reviewed conference or journal papers whose venue was visible in a result or an API record (NeurIPS, AAAI, SIGIR, KDD, ACL/EMNLP/NAACL/EACL/TACL, ICLR, CAIN, KDIR, ICIS, TOSEM, TOIS, Journal of Empirical Legal Studies, MAKE, Applied Sciences, BISE, Knowledge and Process Management, Computer Standards & Interfaces), plus preprints that are central to the enterprise-RAG evaluation debate (unbiased GraphRAG evaluation, BM25 scaling study, Dell deployment reports, EnterpriseRAG and EnterpriseRAG-Bench, WixQA, judge-reliability study). Preprints are labelled as such, and a published version is noted when an API showed one.
- Benchmarks and datasets whose hosting location was visible, to classify reachable (GitHub, Hugging Face, Zenodo, figshare) versus unreachable (publisher supplements) hosts.
- Surveys and literature reviews only as pointers; their claims are not used as findings, except the Applied Sciences SLR whose descriptive statistics are quoted as that review's own counts.
- Interview and experience-report studies, because the area's practitioner evidence is thin and they are the only sources on how organizations actually evaluate RAG.

Dropped:
- Vendor, consultancy and platform blogs (agileinfoways, smarttechlabs, atolio, onyx insights, lushbinary, aivanguard, ailearningguides, metafiedlab, merciv, futureagi, venturebeat, medium, genzeon, wz-it, ewance, runpod, zartis, kore, tianpan, beancount, adaline, gigaspaces, vectorize, dev.to, digitaldividedata). Their cost-per-query, "73% of failures are retrieval", "72-80% deployment failure rate" and "+11 points for fine-tuning plus RAG" figures are not reported as evidence anywhere in this file.
- Patents (USPTO), student theses (Lund, IICT-BAS), aggregator and mirror pages (themoonlight, alphaxiv, papers.cool, awesomepapers, sotaverified, emergentmind, takara, athina, catalyzex, sourcepulse); aggregators were used only to confirm a paper or repository exists, never as a source of findings.
- Off-scope domains: medical and biomedical RAG benchmarks (RAGCare-QA, biomedical RAG benchmark, photocatalyst materials), multimodal RAG (mmRAG, MM-BizRAG, multimodal benchmark 10.1145/3746027.3755625), code-generation RAG poisoning, FMEA knowledge graphs, materials discovery, XR interfaces, Qur'anic research review.
- Results whose snippet or record contained no interpretable finding (conference information pages, author index pages, call-for-papers pages).

Caveat on venue filters: WebSearch's allowed_domains was not honored for aisel.aisnet.org (query 6) and pubsonline.informs.org (query 7) and only partly for sciencedirect.com, onlinelibrary.wiley.com and dl.acm.org. The absence of INFORMS results therefore says nothing about INFORMS coverage; the one AISeL item (ICIS 2025) was found through an unfiltered query (#23, #24), not through the filter.

## Synthesis

### Agreements
- Retrieval, parsing and data preparation dominate failure. The experience report (Barnett et al.: FP1-FP3 are retrieval and consolidation failures), the enterprise benchmark (HERB: "retrieval as the main bottleneck"; the best standard pipeline scores 20.61 while the same model with the full product context scores up to 76.55 and with oracle evidence up to 85.76), the real-document benchmark (UDA: parsing and retrieval matter most), both interview studies (Brehme et al.: data preprocessing is the key challenge; Mueller et al.: new data-quality dimensions concentrate in the extraction and transformation stages) and RGB (negative rejection and integration weak) point the same way from different methods.
- Simple, cheap configurations are stronger than the "advanced RAG" framing implies. DOS RAG matches multi-stage pipelines under matched budgets (Laitenberger et al.); in HERB the dense+BM25 hybrid beats RAPTOR, GraphRAG, HippoRAG-2 and proposition-graph RAG; BM25 alone leads every tier above about 10 million corpus tokens (Wang et al.); fusion gains disappear after re-ranking and truncation (Medrano et al.); large LLM bi-encoders add latency for modest gains (Abdallah et al.); GraphRAG gains shrink under debiased judging (Zeng et al.) and reverse on single-hop questions (Han et al.).
- Evaluation is the weakest link, and practice knows it. Barnett et al. found automated evaluation more pessimistic than human raters and concluded that testing is only possible at runtime; Packowski et al. report that benchmark techniques were not useful for novel user questions; Brehme et al. found 8 of 13 companies evaluating manually and only 2 with LLM-based automation; Norman et al. show exact-match judge validation overstates agreement by 33-41 points of kappa; Zeng et al. attribute part of GraphRAG's reported gains to evaluation bias; Li et al. show a single wording change flips a synthetic RAG-vs-LC result from 80 vs 65 to 5 vs 69.
- Abstention is unsolved. RGB (negative rejection), HERB (unanswerable detection between 6% and 64% depending on model, with a visible trade-off against answerable accuracy), EnterpriseRAG (26.8% holistic compliance under knowledge gaps and conflicts) and Magesh et al. (17-33% hallucination in commercial legal RAG tools) agree that systems answer when they should not.
- Long context does not remove the need for retrieval design. LC wins on average when the corpus fits (Li et al.) but degrades mid-context (Liu et al.) and with hard negatives as k grows (Jin et al.); order-preserving retrieval (DOS RAG, OP-RAG) and routing (Self-Route) recur as the practical compromise; cost is the deciding variable in every one of these papers.

### Contradictions or tensions
- Graph RAG. Edge et al. report substantial gains for global sensemaking; Han et al. find complementary strengths (GraphRAG only on multi-hop); GraphRAG-Bench's abstract states GraphRAG "frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on many real-world tasks"; in HERB GraphRAG is the worst standard method on average (10.31) yet the best at recognizing unanswerable queries (63.41%); Zeng et al. say the gains are moderate once evaluation is debiased. The disagreement is partly task type (global summarization vs factual QA) and partly evaluation protocol.
- Lexical vs dense vs hybrid. HERB's best standard pipeline is a hybrid; Wang et al. report BM25 alone leading at scale with dense retrieval "less accurate"; Medrano et al. find multi-query fusion no better than a single query once budgets are fixed. Offline recall, scale, and end-to-end evidence availability under a fixed context budget are different quantities, and few papers report all three.
- Long context vs RAG. Li et al.: LC beats RAG by 3.6-13.1 points when the context fits; HERB: even with product-scoped context the best LC model is 23 points below its oracle score and open models collapse (Llama-3.1-405B: 18.20); Barnett et al.'s lesson that 8K beat 4K is explicitly "contrary to prior work" on GPT-3.5. Which regime an organization is in depends on corpus size and cost constraints that benchmark papers rarely state.
- Fine-tuning vs RAG. Ovadia et al. and Lakatos et al. find RAG beats unsupervised/domain fine-tuning; Lakatos et al. add that combining them can degrade performance, while Barnett et al. cite end-to-end training as a remedy for bespoke pipelines. Model scale and metric choice (lexical overlap in Lakatos et al.) appear to drive the difference; no enterprise study compares them under matched cost.
- Cost. Practitioners rate cost and scalability as low-priority while prototyping (Brehme et al.), whereas the research results that matter most for architecture choice (Li, Wang, Abdallah, Medrano) are cost-accuracy trade-offs. The research agenda and the practitioner agenda are currently out of phase.
- Judge reliability vs judge usage. HERB generates its data with GPT-4o and judges with GPT-4o; Dell's case-aware judge paper exists because generic judge metrics "provide ambiguous signals"; Norman et al. show production judges can be highly consistent and position-biased at the same time. Nearly every enterprise benchmark in this scan nevertheless reports LLM-judge scores as the headline metric.

### Methodological weaknesses
- Synthetic enterprise corpora (HERB, EnterpriseRAG-Bench, DataMorgana-generated sets, EnterpriseRAG's simulated failure modes) stand in for real internal data; realism of injected noise and cross-document coherence is asserted by construction, and HERB withholds its workflow specifications, so realism cannot be independently audited (visible in HERB's limitations section).
- LLM systems are judged by LLM judges without chance-corrected validation (Norman et al.); where human anchoring exists it is small (HERB: 50 error cases per model; Barnett: 40 flagged issues; RAGTruth and MTRAG are the exceptions with large human label sets but are not enterprise corpora).
- Proprietary evaluations (Dell fusion and judge papers; Packowski et al.; Magesh et al.'s closed tools; the Computer Standards & Interfaces industrial case) cannot be reproduced; the reproducibility preprints found in query 22 report that closed-model drift and unreleased prompts already undermine replication of published RAG methods.
- The practitioner evidence rests on two interview studies with 13 and 16 participants, both European and purposively sampled; neither measures outcomes.
- Public QA datasets carry training-data leakage risk that Li et al. acknowledge and only partially control; scale is rarely varied among the papers seen (Wang et al. is the exception).
- Cost, latency and token budget are reported by a minority (Li, Laitenberger, Wang, Abdallah, Medrano); benchmark papers mostly report accuracy alone.
- Lexical-overlap metrics (ROUGE/BLEU/METEOR in Lakatos et al.; ROUGE for summarization in Li et al.) persist for generative answers.
- All benchmarks seen are static snapshots. None evaluates a corpus that changes over time, although Barnett et al. (validation only in operation), Brehme et al. (re-evaluate after every data change; continuous learning rated important) and Mueller et al. (DQ issues propagate through stages) all describe RAG quality as a moving target. CRUD-RAG's update/delete scenarios are the closest construct seen in this scan.
- Within-paper inconsistencies exist (Barnett et al.: 15,000 vs 4,017 documents; Karakurt & Akbulut: 77 vs 63 primary studies between preprint and journal version), which argues for verifying numbers against full texts rather than abstracts.

### Unresolved questions
- Under matched cost (tokens, latency, index construction) and validated judging, when does any "advanced" component (graph index, fusion, multi-stage, agentic search, LLM retriever) pay off on enterprise-style corpora of realistic size?
- How well do offline retrieval metrics predict end-to-end answer quality once re-ranking and truncation budgets are fixed (Trappolini et al.'s UDCG and Medrano et al.'s Hit@10 collapse point in opposite directions about what to measure)?
- Can small, CPU-runnable judges (fine-tuned classifiers on RAGTruth-style labels, ARES-style PPI with a few hundred labels, embedding-based checks) reach validated, chance-corrected agreement with human experts on enterprise QA?
- How should RAG over an evolving corpus be validated in operation, and which of Mueller et al.'s 15 DQ dimensions actually predict downstream answer failures?
- What is the organizational cost of confident wrong answers versus abstentions, given that abstention rates vary tenfold across models on the same benchmark (HERB)?
- What does the IS/KM literature contribute empirically? This scan found one IS-conference paper (ICIS 2025, data-quality dimensions), one BISE catchword, one KPM adoption survey and one Applied Sciences SLR; no IS-journal field study of organizational outcomes of RAG surfaced, but the AISeL and INFORMS domain filters were not honored, so absence may be a search artifact.

## Candidate research gaps

- Evaluation-validity gap for enterprise RAG benchmarks. Evidence: HERB, EnterpriseRAG, EnterpriseRAG-Bench and the Dell judge paper all rely on LLM judges; Norman et al. show exact-match validation overstates judge quality and that consistency coexists with position bias; Zeng et al. show reported gains shrink under debiased judging; Barnett et al. found automated and human verdicts diverge. What would disprove it: an enterprise RAG benchmark paper reporting chance-corrected human-judge agreement (kappa), position-bias audits and judge-swap sensitivity for its headline results.
- Cost-matched comparison gap. Evidence: advanced components are evaluated at a single corpus size with unbounded budgets; the few cost-matched studies (Laitenberger, Wang, Medrano, Abdallah, Li) each find the simple option competitive or better. What would disprove it: cost-matched (tokens, latency, index build) comparisons on real enterprise corpora across several scales showing consistent gains for graph, fusion or agentic retrieval.
- Lab-to-practice gap in evaluation and data quality. Evidence: 11 of 13 practitioners evaluate manually or not at all (Brehme et al.), IBM reports benchmarks useless for novel questions (Packowski et al.), DQ frameworks do not cover RAG (Mueller et al.), the Applied Sciences SLR calls it a "lab-to-market gap". What would disprove it: field studies in organizations showing automated RAG evaluation adopted with measured agreement to expert judgment, or design-science artifacts for RAG data quality validated in deployment.
- Operational/temporal gap. Evidence: every benchmark seen is a static snapshot; Barnett et al. say validation is only feasible in operation; Brehme et al.'s participants re-evaluate after each data change by hand; CRUD-RAG is the only benchmark seen in this scan with update/delete scenarios. What would disprove it: longitudinal evaluations of RAG systems over corpora that change, with regression detection, published in any venue.
- Abstention and consequence gap. Evidence: unanswerable detection ranges from 6% to 64% across models on HERB; RGB's negative-rejection weakness persists; Magesh et al. measure 17-33% hallucination in professional tools whose vendors claimed none. What would disprove it: studies linking calibrated abstention to measured organizational outcomes (time saved, error cost) rather than to benchmark accuracy.
- IS/KM theory gap. Evidence: the only IS-venue empirical item found is an interview-based DQ paper; the KM-venue item is a UTAUT2 adoption survey in universities; conceptual pieces (BISE catchword) dominate. What would disprove it: IS-journal or ICIS/ECIS empirical studies of RAG-enabled knowledge management outcomes in organizations, which this scan may have missed because the AISeL filter failed.
