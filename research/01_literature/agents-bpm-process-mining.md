## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Kept:
- Work on LLMs or LLM-based agents applied to BPM lifecycle tasks (modeling, mining, predictive monitoring, execution and automation), on the governance of such agents in organizations, and on benchmarks that evaluate agents on enterprise or business-process tasks, where the record appeared in a search or API response received in this run and at least its abstract could be read (one exception, Kaltenpoth et al., is kept at metadata/snippet depth because the BPM 2025 main-track result it reports is directly relevant).
- Peer-reviewed venues preferred (ACM TMIS, Information Systems, Process Science, SoSyM, BPM conference and forum, ICPM workshops, ICML, NeurIPS, TMLR). Preprints are included when central and are labelled, with their acceptance status copied from the arXiv comment field where one exists; where verify_refs.py found a published version (Weytjens and Weber; TheAgentCompany; Cemri et al.; Pyrih et al.), the row records the published venue and DOI.
- Negative or sobering results (argmax baseline vs LLMs, hallucination study, failure taxonomy, low benchmark success rates) were deliberately sought.

Dropped:
- Vendor blogs, consultancy pages, news items and paper-mirror sites (alphaxiv, emergentmind, papers.cool, beancount, opentrain, fugumt); their numbers (e.g. "70-95% of agents fail") are not used.
- USPTO patents, theses (except as noted in the supplementary list), and papers whose only relation to the area is the phrase "workflow" (e.g. astrophysics or financial time-series agent workflows).
- Items seen only as a URL without a readable title or abstract (e.g. arXiv 2412.05958, 2604.14034) are mentioned in the search log but not recorded as papers.
- Reviews were not opened in full; the IPM 2026 review and the Journal of Computer Science 2025 review are listed as supplementary items only.

## Synthesis

### Agreements

- Across the manifestos (Dumas et al. 2023; Calvanese et al. 2026; Dumas, Milani and Chapela-Campa 2025) and the empirical modeling and mining papers (Kourani et al. 2026; Rebmann et al. 2025; Berti et al. 2025), there is agreement that LLMs are useful for text-facing BPM tasks (model generation, model comprehension, question answering over process abstractions) but that LLMs alone do not give reliable, process-aware execution. The 2026 manifesto states this explicitly ("LLMs alone are insufficient"); Kaltenpoth et al.'s 16%-to-1% failure reduction with process rules (visible only as a snippet) and Kirchdorfer et al.'s organizational-memory argument point the same way from the engineering side.
- The enterprise agent benchmarks agree on the magnitude of the capability gap: TheAgentCompany (best 30.3% full completion), CRMArena-Pro (about 58% single-turn, 35% multi-turn), WorkArena ("considerable gap"), Thinkingbox (steep pass^k drops), EntCollabBench and World of Workflows (failures in delegation, hidden side effects). They also agree on where agents fail: communication with colleagues, document and UI handling, multi-turn information gathering, policy adherence and confidentiality, rather than isolated reasoning.
- Evaluation design is converging on executable, state-based checks (TheAgentCompany checkpoints, Agent-Diff state diffs, E-Bench database diffs, Thinkingbox backend-state checks) and away from LLM-judged free text, while the PM-specific benchmark (PM-LLM-Benchmark) still relies on an LLM judge and its authors flag the resulting evaluation biases.

### Contradictions or tensions

- Optimistic early results (Grohs et al. 2023: LLMs "comparably to or better than existing solutions" without prompt engineering; Berti and Qafari 2023: "robust understanding of key process mining abstractions") sit against later controlled studies: Rebmann et al. 2025 find out-of-the-box LLMs "struggle" with semantics-aware tasks until fine-tuned; Kourani, Antonov et al. 2025 find models revert to standard process schemas against explicit evidence; Weytjens and Weber 2026 find that for next-activity prediction a counting-based argmax baseline "matches or approaches" billion-parameter LLMs on most of seven real logs. The resolution appears to be task-dependent (text-to-model vs log-grounded prediction) and setting-dependent (Padella et al. report LLM gains only with about 100 traces), but no paper in this scan tests that resolution directly.
- The manifestos frame agents as "first-class participants" governed by normative frames, whereas the enterprise benchmarks that actually measure agents (TheAgentCompany, WorkArena, CRMArena-Pro) contain no process model, frame or conformance notion; conversely, the process-mining benchmarks (PM-LLM-Benchmark, Rebmann et al.) evaluate LLM answers, not agents acting in a process. The Yang et al. 2026 agenda and Thinkingbox's policy-conditioned workflows are the closest items to bridging this, and both are 2026 preprints.
- Practitioners expect compliance benefits from agents (Vu et al. 2025), while CRMArena-Pro measures "near-zero inherent confidentiality awareness" and reports that prompting for it costs task performance. Expectation and measurement therefore point in opposite directions on governance.
- Venue metadata disagree: Semantic Scholar/DBLP list TheAgentCompany and Cemri et al. as NeurIPS 2025 while the arXiv records still say "Preprint"; Crossref gives print year 2026 for BPM 2025 proceedings chapters. The table records both.

### Methodological weaknesses

- Conceptual work dominates the BPM side: two manifestos, two vision papers and a practitioner study whose 22 interviewees had no hands-on experience with agentic AI. Empirical evidence about deployed LLM agents inside business processes was not found at the targeted IS venues (the only AIS items are a typology, a research-in-progress paper and a consultant-role evaluation).
- Benchmarks are mostly vendor- or lab-authored with synthetic or introspectively created tasks (stated by Xu et al.; inferred for CRMArena-Pro and WorkArena), small task counts (19 to 175), two scaffolds, and no human-professional baseline; several results are reported only in preprints (Thinkingbox, Agent-Diff, EntCollabBench, World of Workflows).
- Evaluation validity: PM-LLM-Benchmark depends on an LLM judge and its authors name evaluation bias as unresolved; TheAgentCompany fixes the evaluator and NPC model to one vendor's LLM; Weytjens and Weber show that simple baselines are often missing from PPM comparisons.
- Reproducibility and compute: fine-tuning results (Rebmann et al.; Pyrih et al.) and frontier-model benchmark runs ($4 per task in TheAgentCompany) are not reproducible on CPU-only hardware; the one Zenodo record found for a PM reasoning-trace dataset (PMLRM-Bench) has been retracted.

### Unresolved questions

- Under which conditions (log size, label semantics, attribute richness) do LLMs add predictive or analytical value over trivial and classical baselines in process mining?
- How should an agent's execution trace be evaluated against a normative process frame, and can conformance checking supply that evaluation (the manifesto's C3 and the BlueSky agenda both ask this; none of the benchmarks implements it)?
- Do LLMs override event-log evidence with pre-trained process schemas in discovery, conformance and root-cause tasks, as they do in text-to-model generation?
- What governance mechanisms (frames, organizational memory, process rules) measurably reduce agent failures, and at what cost to task completion (the CRMArena-Pro trade-off)?
- Can instruction-tuned small open-weight models reach the "satisfactory" level on PM tasks that PM-LLM-Benchmark reports only for larger models?

## Candidate research gaps

- **Process-aware evaluation of enterprise agents.** Evidence: the 2026 manifesto lists "Benchmarking and Evaluation Frameworks for APM" as an open cross-cutting challenge; TheAgentCompany, WorkArena and CRMArena-Pro evaluate outcomes or UI states but contain no process model, frame or conformance measure; PM-LLM-Benchmark evaluates LLM answers, not agent behaviour. Would be disproved by a published benchmark (not only an agenda such as Yang et al. 2026) in which agent traces are scored by conformance to a normative or operational process model, with act/defer/ask/refuse outcomes.
- **When LLM semantics help predictive process monitoring.** Evidence: Weytjens and Weber (argmax matches LLMs on most of seven logs) versus Padella et al. (LLM wins with about 100 traces) and Rebmann et al. (fine-tuning needed). Would be disproved by a study that reports trivial, classical and LLM baselines on the same public logs under varying training-set sizes and shows a consistent LLM advantage, or none.
- **Evidence fidelity of LLMs on log-grounded tasks.** Evidence: Kourani, Antonov et al. show knowledge-driven hallucination in text-to-model generation; no equivalent controlled study on event-log inputs (atypical or shuffled logs) was found. Would be disproved by a paper that measures how often LLM-based discovery or analysis contradicts the log in favour of a standard process schema.
- **Field evidence on governing agents in business processes.** Evidence: Vu et al.'s interviewees had no practical experience; the IS-venue hits are a typology (ICIS 2024) and a research-in-progress paper (ECIS 2026); Kaltenpoth et al.'s process-rule result is the only empirical governance mechanism found and only its snippet was readable. Would be disproved by case or field studies of deployed LLM agents in organizational processes with measured compliance, failure or rework outcomes.
- **Mapping agent failure modes to process deviations.** Evidence: Cemri et al.'s 14-mode taxonomy and the agent-observability preprints (COMPASS; arXiv 2505.20127) exist separately from conformance-checking deviation types; no paper linking them was found. Would be disproved by a method paper that classifies agent-trace deviations with process-mining constructs and relates them to reliability outcomes.
- **Small open-weight models for process mining under CPU constraints.** Evidence: PM-LLM-Benchmark reports tiny models "still inadequate"; Pyrih et al. show instruction tuning helps on some tasks only; Busch and Leopold focus on small models but remain a preprint. Would be disproved by a benchmark showing that 1-4B instruction-tuned models reach the level of larger models on semantics-aware tasks.

## Limitations of this scan

- Access depth: 3 of 16 main rows are full-text (Calvanese et al. 2026; Vu et al. 2025; Xu et al. TheAgentCompany), 12 are abstract-level, 1 is metadata/snippet (Kaltenpoth et al. 2025, whose 16%-to-1% figure comes from a search-result summary and should be re-checked against the chapter). In the supplementary list, 3 items are metadata-only (Padella et al. BPM Forum chapter; Comuzzi et al. CAiSE 2026; the S2-citation-list items). Findings columns quote only what was visible.
- Search engine coverage: 24 WebSearch queries, 4 in extended mode. Domain filters leaked arXiv results heavily, and the INFORMS/Wiley filter returned nothing from those publishers, so coverage of Information Systems Research, Management Science and ISJ for this area is effectively untested. No HICSS or CAiSE main-track LLM-agent paper surfaced except Comuzzi et al.
- API coverage: Crossref had no abstracts for most Springer LNBIP/LNCS chapters; Semantic Scholar answered 429 for five lookups on the first pass and the DOI lookup for the Vidgof et al. chapter returned 404 (recovered via arXiv ID). OpenAlex was used only inside verify_refs.py.
- Citation chasing: the Semantic Scholar citations endpoint returns at most 100 records per call and I read only the first page sorted by citation count for Dumas et al. 2023 (100 of more) and TheAgentCompany (100 of more); the 2026 manifesto had 15 citing papers, all read. References were read for all three. Citing papers of 2026 preprints are naturally sparse, so forward chasing favours older work.
- Sites that refused fetches: link.springer.com chapter pages returned a "Client Challenge" page to curl (Crossref used instead); api.github.com refused repository metadata without add_repo; the Zenodo record for PMLRM-Bench is deleted. dl.acm.org, sciencedirect.com and ieeexplore.ieee.org were not fetched directly, in line with the environment facts. WebFetch was not used for arXiv, AIS eLibrary or 4TU; curl from Bash reached them.
- Dates: all searches and API calls were made on 2026-10-03; venue and year fields reflect what Crossref, the arXiv export API and Semantic Scholar returned on that day and disagree in a few cases noted in the table.
