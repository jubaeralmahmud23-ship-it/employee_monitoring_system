## Reasons and notes

- **S01** (10.1007/s12525-023-00680-1). I0 = N: Fundamentals article (conceptual overview of generative AI): no evaluation of any system. Note: Quoted ChatGPT (GPT-4, 'Mar 23 Version') epigraph on p.1 is not an evaluation.
- **S02** (10.1007/s10796-026-10804-5). I0 = N: Cross-sectional survey of 143 knowledge workers' self-reported GenAI use, dependence and perceived skill erosion; no LLM output or performance is evaluated. Note: The paper does report CIs and p-values, but for a behavioural survey model, which is outside the codebook's object (evaluation of an LLM-based system).
- **S03** (10.1007/s12525-024-00746-8). I0 = B-T: Design science study of a synthetic-data ecosystem; the evaluated generative models are GMM, CTGAN, TVAE and TimeGAN for tabular transaction data (no language model); quantitative evaluation via XGBoost ROC AUC on a 30% holdout. Headline: "While CTGAN (ROC AUC score 0.59) performed a little better, TVAE (ROC AUC score 0.89) excelled" (p.13 (results; the abstract has no quantitative comparison)). Note: Borderline-technology case: would enter only if the frame's inclusion criterion is widened to non-language generative models. Code and tuning described in a GitHub repository (footnotes 2 and 4, p.13). No uncertainty reported for the AUC comparisons.
- **S04** (10.1007/s12525-025-00812-9). I0 = N: Single-case study of the Amazon Alexa ecosystem through service-dominant logic; the only model evaluated is a graph attention network (GNN) for skill complementarity, not an LLM or other language/generative model; ChatGPT appears only as an example of an assistant platform (the term hit). Headline: "The model achieved 84.33% accuracy, effectively distinguishing between complementary and non-complementary pairs." (p.11 (results; the abstract has no quantitative comparison)). Note: Out of scope for the audit (no LLM evaluated), but an illustration of an n inconsistency the audit would catch: 15% test split = 3,813 pairs, while TN+TP+FP+FN = 11,185+10,247+1,560+2,423 = 25,415 (p.11). The paper reports t-tests (p < 0.05) for the label construction, not for model performance.
- **S05** (10.1007/s10796-023-10375-9). I0 = N: Bibliometric study of over 5,000 research articles on conversational agents (keywords, topics, author networks); no system is evaluated.
- **S06** (10.1007/s12599-025-00974-y). I0 = B-H: Between-subjects human experiment (n = 75 participants): GPT-3.5 brainstorming support as treatment; outcomes are human and human+GLM dyad brainstorming performance and cognitive load, not an evaluation of the LLM on a dataset. Headline: "Mann-Whitney-U test, which showed a significant difference (U = 475.0, p = 0.015) between the human-only (mdn = 5) and human+GLM (mdn = 4) condition" (§4.1 p.12 (the abstract has no numeric comparison besides n = 75)). Note: Coded for information only (borderline human-subject experiment). The paper already reports significance tests and a sensitivity analysis; the binomial survival models do not apply to count or rating outcomes.
- **S07** (10.1007/s12599-026-00998-y). I0 = N: Qualitative study: 30 expert interviews with software development practitioners on GenAI opportunities and pitfalls; no system is evaluated quantitatively.
- **S08** (10.1007/s10796-025-10581-7). I0 = N: Conceptual and historical review proposing a sociotechnical research agenda for GenAI in IS; no evaluation.
- **S09** (10.1007/s10796-024-10503-z). I0 = Y: Quantitative evaluation of active-learning uncertainty measures for fine-tuned transformer-encoder language models (BERT-base, RoBERTa-base) on seven public NLP datasets. Headline: "First Peak clipping ... slightly outperforms top-k clipping throughout all results (Table 3: average relative gain in acc_last5 of 0.98% vs 0.95% for BERT)" (§5.1 p.10, Table 3 p.10 (the abstract has no numeric comparison)). Note: Headline gains are reported as relative percentages pooled over datasets and seeds; the two accuracies being compared are not reported, so no binomial interval can be computed from the paper. Per-dataset accuracies appear only as boxplots (Fig. 5).
- **S10** (10.1007/s12525-026-00915-x). I0 = N: Qualitative study: 15 interviews in two German tech firms plus analysis of handbooks and workflow artefacts, deriving a nine-step oversight protocol (OP-9); no system is evaluated quantitatively.
- **A01** (2024.naacl-industry.1). Headline: "achieving an impressive speedup in latency and throughput up to 2.28 times" (abstract p.1; §4.2.1 p.5 (GP-B and HP: latency reduction 51-56%, throughput 2.06-2.28x)). Note: Frame decision for the author: industry tracks contain systems-efficiency papers (timing claims). They are outside the binomial survival models even if included; timing variance (repetitions) is the relevant uncertainty and is not reported here (no repetitions or spread stated).

Headline: "Table 1: DistilQwen2.5-3B-R1 16.7 vs Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct 6.7 on AIME2024 (first comparison in the results; §4.2 text: 'consistent and substantial gains across multiple benchmarks')" (Table 1 p.5 (the abstract has no numeric comparison)). - **A03** (2025.emnlp-industry.173). I0 = Y: Retrievers distilled from an LLM (synthetic queries) and a VLM (ranking scores), evaluated on industrial retrieval benchmarks; RAG pipelines with Llama-3.1-8B evaluated with RAGAs. Headline: "Our retrievers outperform all baseline models across all evaluation metrics." (§4.2 p.5 referring to Table 1 p.3 (the abstract has no numeric comparison)). Note: The headline sentence is not literally true in Table 1: Ours (BGE-M3) ties ColQwen2.5-3B on MRR@5/@10 and nDCG@5/@10 (87.9/88.2/90.0/90.5 both); it is higher only on HIT@5 (96.0 vs 95.9) and HIT@10 (97.6 vs 97.4). The paper's only test (t-test, p = 0.171) checks run-to-run variation of its own model, not the comparison.
- **A04** (2025.emnlp-industry.149). I0 = Y: New embodied-planning safety benchmark (VestaBench-VH, 100 tasks; VestaBench-B50, 50 tasks) on which LLM agents (GPT-4.1-Mini, Qwen3-32B with Direct/ReAct/Reflexion/Critic) are evaluated by success and safety rates. Headline: "ReAct demonstrated approximately 5% and 10% improvements in both macro and micro success and safety rates on VestaBench-VH" (§4.2 RQ1 p.5 with Table 2 p.5 (the abstract has no numeric comparison)). Note: Possible n inconsistency the audit would flag: App. B.2 states 135 success goals in VestaBench-VH, but four of the five GPT-4.1-Mini micro success rates in Table 2 (9.02, 48.87, 57.14, 61.65) equal k/133 to two decimals (k = 12, 65, 76, 82) and none equals k/135; 63.15 matches 84/133 only if truncated. Checked by arithmetic in this run, not confirmed with the authors.
- **A05** (2025.naacl-industry.49). I0 = Y: LLM-based risk-detection (guardrail) models Granite-Guardian-3.0-2B/8B evaluated against Llama-Guard and ShieldGemma on public prompt/response harmfulness benchmarks and the TRUE groundedness benchmark. Headline: "Granite-Guardian-3.0-8B consistently shows higher scores in both F1 and AUC ... achieves higher aggregate scores (Table 1: aggregate F1 0.758 vs 0.723 for Llama-Guard-2-8B, the best competitor)" (§5 p.6, Table 1 p.6 (the abstract has no numeric comparison)). Note: The evaluated models are themselves LLM judges; their validation against human-labelled benchmarks uses F1/AUC, with no chance-corrected agreement, which the judge-validation items do not capture because item 7 refers to judges used to score the evaluated system.
- **A06** (2026.acl-industry.14). I0 = Y: Linear-probe uncertainty estimates for LLM judges (Llama, Qwen, J1, Llama-4-Scout, GPT-OSS-20B) evaluated for calibration (Kuiper, ECE) on PPE, JudgeBench and RewardBench against verbalized, self-consistency and majority baselines. Headline: "probes achieve superior calibration compared to existing methods with ≈ 10x computational savings" (abstract p.1). Note: Headline metrics (compute ratio, Kuiper, ECE) are not proportions, so the binomial survival models do not apply even though n is reported. The paper is itself about uncertainty of LLM judges.
- **A07** (2026.acl-industry.34). I0 = Y: RL post-training method (REPO) for a Qwen3-32B negotiation agent, evaluated by expert consensus on 30 online conversations and 45 curated bad cases against SFT/DPO/PPO/GRPO, plus a production A/B test on 9,653 conversations. Headline: "REPO improves average dialogue rating to 4.63 (+0.33 over GRPO)" (abstract p.1; §6 p.5). Note: The abstract's first comparison is a mean rating (not a proportion); its second is a proportion on n = 30 (run as the alternative cell). The A/B test is the only claim with a significance test.
- **A08** (2026.acl-industry.12). I0 = Y: New document-packet-splitting benchmark (five variants) on which multimodal LLMs (Claude Sonnet/Haiku 4.5, DeepSeek V3.1, Gemma 3 27B, Qwen 3 VL 235B) are evaluated. Headline: "Qwen 3 consistently achieves the highest Packet scores (0.9238 - 0.9492) ... Claude Sonnet, Claude Haiku, and DeepSeek form a competitive second tier (Packet >0.90)" (§8 p.5 with Table 2 p.6 (the abstract has no numeric comparison)). Note: The only pilot paper whose design reports a cluster structure (pages within packets, pages per packet in Table 8); used for the clustered model under both Small and Large assumptions.
- **A09** (2025.emnlp-industry.146). I0 = Y: Inference-time method (CaR) for large reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/14B, QwQ-32B) evaluated on MATH-500, AMC-2023, GPQA-Diamond and AIME 2023-2025 against plain CoT. Headline: "CaR improves answer accuracy by up to +13.3% (abstract); 'On challenging datasets like GPQA-Diamond and AIME-2025, CaR improves performance by up to +13.3' (§4.2)" (abstract p.1; §4.2 p.5; Table 1 p.5 (Distill-Qwen-14B, AIME-2025)). Note: 'Up to +13.3' in the abstract matches two cells (14B AIME-2025; QwQ-32B AIME-2024); Table 1 also contains +15.0 (7B AMC-2023), so the abstract's maximum refers to the 'challenging datasets' named in §4.2. Some values are not consistent with the standard benchmark sizes (QwQ-32B AIME-2023 69.7 is not k/30; GPQA 5.7 is not k/198), which suggests averaging or different n; not resolvable from the paper.

I0 = Y: T5-initialised multimodal encoder-decoder transformer (MQMA) evaluated on OCR-VQA, TextVQA, ST-VQA and DocVQA against an in-house single-question baseline (SQSA, DocFormerv2-style) and published systems.
