### 6. Information Systems Journal (ISJ)

Realistic-tier reasoning: the explicit theory requirement and qualitative lean make ISJ a stretch for pure measurement work; idea 3 (if framed through impression-management or socio-technical theory) is the best fit, and idea 6 could be a Research Opinion about evaluation practice rather than a Research Article.

## (c) What a top-journal submission requires (synthesised from author guidelines actually read)

Sources: JMIS info for contributors; JAIS about and policies pages; EJIS editors' guidelines; ISJ manuscript types; CAIS policies; ISR and MISQ snippets (third-party, marked). All read 2026-10-05.

1. Contribution expectations. JMIS requires "a significant novel contribution to the field of information systems". ISJ Research Articles "must normally reference theory, whether through validating, building, challenging, refuting or refining one or more theories" and authors should declare their epistemological and ontological stance. EJIS accepts "original analysis of a salient policy issue, the exploitation of new data, and/or the innovative development and application of theory", which is the most measurement-friendly wording among the six. JAIS wants "innovative, interesting and rigorously developed conceptual and empirical contributions" and "encourages theory based multi- or inter-disciplinary research". Implication: a measurement or audit study must be wrapped in a theoretical or policy argument (what the finding changes about how IS scholars or organisations should reason), not just a result table.
2. Length. JMIS 50 pages for the complete manuscript (appendices online); EJIS 9,000-12,000 words including references; ISJ 10,000 words of main text; ISR Research Notes <= 20 pages with <= 8 pages of figures/tables (snippet); MISQ Research Article 50 pages all-inclusive, Research Note about half (third-party). The 20-page ISR Research Note and MISQ Research Note are the formats that best suit a focused measurement study.
3. Review model and timelines. Double-blind at JMIS ("two or more stages"), EJIS (desk review about two weeks, three reviewers, about 12 weeks to decision) and, by snippet, ISR and MISQ; JAIS uses a developmental Senior Editor model with "relatively short cycle times"; BISE reports a median 25 days to first decision; CAIS 61 days average for peer-reviewed papers. None of the six Group 1 journals publishes an acceptance rate on a page received (the 7-10% figure for ISR is third-party and should not be cited).
4. Data and instruments. JAIS requires that empirical datasets be available on request, that quantitative studies provide covariance/correlation matrices and descriptives, and allows Senior Editors to request full datasets. EJIS encourages quantitative authors to make data available for replication and will host it. JMIS requires survey instruments to be supplied to referees. For the eight ideas this is an advantage: all use public data and can ship full replication packages.
5. Open access and fees. Green self-archiving is stated by JAIS (no embargo), EJIS (on acceptance) and CAIS; JMIS mentions no fees; AIS journals charge USD 75 only when no author is an AIS member. No Group 1 journal requires an APC.
6. Preprints. Only TMLR, ARR and ACM (via the JRC announcement) state preprint permissions explicitly; none of the IS journals' pages received address arXiv or SSRN posting, so the author should ask the editor before posting a preprint of an IS submission.
7. Current calls. ISR's GenAI methods-of-inquiry special issue (deadline 2026-09-07) has passed; ISF has five open AI-and-society collections with deadlines between 2026-10-30 and 2027-09-15; Process Science has two open collections; no 2026 call was found at BISE, DSS, JAIS or I&M on AI evaluation specifically.
